# The Holy Heretics

### The same unmediated meeting, turned on the institution — and the metered practice the frames series said was missing

*Second of the `readings`, and the direct sequel to [the holy daimon](/the-holy-daimon/). That post read
Frater Acher's first volume and found a guide that is **met** rather than mediated — unowned, pointed
outward, no seat in it, and no check outside the practitioner either. This is the volume Acher wrote next,
and it turns the same claim onto the **institution**: the antagonism is not between one experience of the
divine and another, but between any unmediated encounter and organised orthodoxy. It is the inverse of
[the owned cosmos](/the-owned-cosmos/) — and it is the **metered practice** that
[the cosmos that refuses](/the-cosmos-that-refuses/) said the apophatic refusal still needed.*

*Status.* This post reads **one** source. The quotations are verbatim from the digital edition, checked
character for character against the file and cited in the notes; the reading of them is an **inference**,
offered as one. **The book's cosmology is not endorsed** — nothing here claims that a daimon or an Olympic
spirit exists. No living author is diagnosed. The criticism below is of a **structure** — orthodoxy as a
single mediated route to the divine — and of no church or people, and nothing here validates, disqualifies
or restricts anyone's practice. As everywhere: describe structures, diagnose no one.

---

## 1. The book, and the cycle it closes

*Holy Heretics* is by Frater Acher, published by Scarlet Imprint in 2022, illustrated by Jose Gabriel
Alegría Sabogal, digital ISBN 978-1-912316-68-7.[^hh] It is the third volume of a cycle, and its
Introduction says so in the same breath as its method:

> "It is best for those genuinely committed to a path of wisdom and personal evolution to **harbour a
> healthy mistrust towards what we think we know and who we think we are**. This is the spirit in which
> this book was conceived, **as the third and final volume of the Holy Daimon cycle**."[^hh]

Its structure is two books, and the structure is the argument.[^st] **BOOK I · MYSTICISM** runs
Pseudo-Dionysius and the path of unknowing; the Heart Prayer, through the Desert Fathers and the Hesychasts;
Symeon the New Theologian, Evagrius Ponticus and Hildegard of Bingen; the *Granum Sinapis*; and the
*Theologia Germanica*. **BOOK II · MAGIC** runs The Call, The Olympic Spirit and The Rite of the Olympic
Spirit — where the reader is handed a working ritual. Each chapter of the first half ends the same way, and
the Introduction says why: "Each chapter, therefore, ends with straightforward instructions on how to approach
your own heart space".[^hh] It is not a history with exercises appended. It is a manual whose historical half
**is** the training.

One counterweight belongs here. The chapter on the Desert Fathers documents what the early Church's turn to
worldly power cost, and its evidence includes the Church Fathers' own anti-Jewish polemic, quoted at length.
**This post does not reproduce those quotations** — what matters is what the book does with them:

> "Let us be mindful where we place our own feet on this journey. What will we use as the basis of our
> ascent? Are we ready to place ourselves in the hands of divinity, rather than placing our feet on the
> face of another man or tradition?"[^feet]

and then states the rule outright: "a genuine mystical path requires neither the debasement and humiliation
of others, nor the debasement of everything that does not fit into the confines of our magical
circle".[^feet] That is this post's finding too: an **inward discipline turned outward as a rule for everyone
else**. The poison is not a property of any people. It is a move a structure makes.

---

## 2. The thesis the book states for itself

The Introduction does not hide its argument. It begins from the epigraph — Sheikh Mahmud Shabistari's
*Gulshan-i Raz*, 1317 CE, whose closing lines end "**Each one speaks the language native to the level he has
reached**, and it is hard to understand what he says" — and cashes it a few lines in: "In this world
where we each possess a unique shard of truth, **hasty judgement is the enemy of wisdom**."[^epi] Then the
thesis:

> "the source of so much hostility in our shared history does not reside in the difference of one man's
> experience of the divine as opposed to another's, but in **the ever antagonistic forces between any
> unmediated experience of divinity and all forms of organised spiritual orthodoxy**. The true mystic —
> like the true magician — irrespective of whether he or she emerges from an Egyptian, Jewish, Christian,
> Muslim, or any other paradigm, **will always be perceived as a threat to organised spiritual
> institutions**."[^thesis]

Note what that does not say. The antagonism is not doctrinal, and no experience is called truer than another:
the hostility is located **between a kind of event and a kind of organisation**. The reason follows: Western
mystics and magicians "all share one thing: **the antisocial stench of the primal *goēs***", and their way of
life "posed a threat to any kind of **codified, mediated and sanitised form of spirituality**. […] **It is in
this direct experience of the spirit that we stand united as holy heretics.**"[^thesis]

**The reading is this post's, and it inverts [the owned cosmos](/the-owned-cosmos/).** That post described a
frame that names an endpoint and stations a mediator at it — and the mediator is not an abuse of the frame,
because a named endpoint is a place and a place is occupiable. *Holy Heretics* describes the same
architecture from the far side. A mediated route is a claim about where the divine comes through; a direct
encounter is evidence that it came through elsewhere. That is not a disagreement, it is a devaluation of the
route. **The heretical charge is not what the mystic says. It is that the mystic came back.**

**And the counterweight, in the body.** That a direct encounter will *always* be perceived as a threat is a
claim by a party who has had one, about what institutions do, and it is not checkable from inside the book. It
is reported as **what the book states about a structure**, and it draws no conclusion about practice: a person
can be inside an institution and have the encounter.

## 3. The seat, inverted — and the distinction the corpus already drew

The book's strongest exhibit is a case, and it is the case the blog's own framing predicts. Symeon the New
Theologian wrote down his experience of the divine light in the tenth century; the book states the
institutional consequence flatly:

> "**If salvation did not need to be mediated by a priest and through the collective experience of church
> service, then the very foundations of the Roman Catholic Church were at risk.** […] [T]o propagate
> spiritual techniques that were open and accessible to the lay practitioner was to implicitly challenge
> the Church's mechanisms of social control and institutional spiritual power."[^symeon]

The outcome is why the book chose him: "the Holy Roman Empire considered Symeon's hymns the work of an
apostate, and **in the West today they have fallen into oblivion**."[^symeon] Against a lineage it sets a
record — the tradition "is not kept, confined or curated by any kind of **manmade orthodoxy**, but by the
account of a personal, deeply transformative experience of divinity's darkness as well as its piercing rays
of light."[^symeon]

**The counterweight is already in the corpus, and it binds here.** [The owned cosmos](/the-owned-cosmos/)
wrote the distinction this post must not lose: what matters is not *mediated versus unmediated* but **open
mediation against closed mediation** — several routes against one, a mediator accountable outside themselves
against a mediator who is the reason you cannot get through any other way. *Holy Heretics* is itself a
mediation: a printed manual **with a prerequisite**. "I would advise against skipping ahead and delving into
the section titled MAGIC. Contrary to common assumptions, **the practice of advanced magic should not precede
but instead follow periods of intense mystical training**."[^hh] So the book's "unmediated" is narrower than
it sounds: the encounter is unmediated in that no office stands at it, but **the route to it is mediated by a
book, and the book sets a gate.** What it is against is a mediation that **closes** — the foundations were at
risk precisely when *the other route worked*.

## 4. The apophatic path, as a metered practice

[The cosmos that refuses](/the-cosmos-that-refuses/) named the **apophatic frame** — a correspondence with no
nameable endpoint — and ended on a finding that hurt: the refusal "is not self-enforcing", because a claim to
stand in the unnamed place is unbeatable. Its conclusion was that the safeguard lives in a **practice**, not a
frame. *Holy Heretics*' opening chapter is that practice, named and cited. Pseudo-Dionysius's *Mystical
Theology* and *On the Divine Names* open the book, and the book states what the negative way is for: rather
than assembling an archive of cognitive insights about divinity, the Areopagite used hymnal prayer to "immerse
oneself entirely into **a state of agnosis**, i.e. not-knowing."[^apo]

> "**Agnosis is a path towards oneness with the superessential divinity; it is a path that never ends**, a
> journey that can never entirely succeed — for if it did, we would lose everything of our own *gestalt*,
> that is to say, the defining features that make us *us*."[^apo]

Then the two sentences that make this the frames series' specification, in the book's own voice:

> "a path of agnosis has no interest in debating spiritual truth with any other confession; for
> philosophical debate would be the precise opposite of its own practice. Most obviously, unlike the
> classical magician we described above, **a path of agnosis is utterly useless to increase personal
> agency or to enlist practical help from other spiritual beings**."[^apo]

**That is a criterion, not a mood, and it is what the refusal needed.** The apophatic move fails when someone
claims the unnamed place as a credential, and this version carries an internal disqualifier: *it does not
increase your agency and it does not get you help.* The claim to have arrived is therefore not merely
discouraged; it is **inconsistent with the practice's stated product**. There follow the exercises: attention
carried down from the lips to the mind to the heart; the *"heart space or heart flame"* as *"the actual crack
between the human and divine world, from which the light comes in"*; the *Theologia Germanica*'s "whyless
(*warumlos*) and wayless (*weglos*) path".[^apo]

**The counterweight is stated by the book itself, up front:** three negative results at once. That is the
opposite of the genre this blog usually meets, and by the book's own account it is *not a method of
protection*.

## 5. The dose

The book's load-bearing structure is not the refusal. It is a **meter**, and it arrives as a quotation before
it arrives as a claim. The Heart Prayer chapter's §V carries Paracelsus as its epigraph:

> "**All things are poison, and nothing is without poison; but the dose makes it that a thing is not a
> poison.** — Paracelsus, *Septem defensiones*"[^dose]

The book then applies it, in the passage where the historical argument and the safety argument turn out to be
one argument: "As wiser, independent practitioners though, we know that **everything is a poison; it is the
dosage that defines its effect**."[^dose] What follows is a hard reading of the desert ascetics — the fasting,
the irons, the graves — and then the sentence that makes it a safety claim rather than a polemic. The failure
was not the extremity. It was the **export**: "[T]he radical attitude required for a period of intense
spiritual retreat was disseminated and propagandised as the new God-given blueprint for all of human society.
[…] [I]t quickly became a new fanatical orthodoxy circumscribing everyone's everyday lives."[^desert] The rule
for a practitioner is the same at the scale of one person:

> "There is no problem – in fact, there is a lot of benefit – in leaning into an extreme lifestyle for a
> period of time. **But when the rituals are done, we centre ourselves again in a more balanced approach to
> the world.**"[^desert]

**This is the safeguard the blog calls a metered pace, named here as a pharmacology.** [The
safeguards](/safeguards/) found the traditions writing gradualism into their practice — *anupubba-sikkhā*, the
lam rim; [the Western column](/the-western-column/) found the same requirement as a syllabus, in Quareia's
"slow, careful steps". *Holy Heretics* states the rule underneath — **the same act is a remedy at one
dose and a poison at another** — and draws the half most manuals omit: the extremity is bounded in time, but
also in **scope**, and the return is part of the operation.

**Counterweight.** The meter has no external setting. The dose is judged by the practitioner, in a book with
no gate, no supervisor and — §6 — no referral: a specification the reader must keep, which is [the cosmos that
refuses](/the-cosmos-that-refuses/)' finding restated.

## 6. What the book warns about, and what is missing

Here is the negative finding, stated as carefully as [the holy daimon](/the-holy-daimon/) stated its own.
**The word "safeguard" does not occur in the digital text of *Holy Heretics*** — a case-insensitive search
returns 0 hits. The clinical referral is likewise absent: no "therapist", no "psychiatrist", no "mental
health", no "psychosis", no "clinical", no "go see" and no "seek help" in any form. The hits for "therapy",
"physician", "medical" and "doctor" are a therapy-room metaphor, the *antidotum Mithridaticum*, historical
physicians (Paracelsus, Celsus, a Pavia court physician), Hildegard's title of "doctor", and a Gospel line
("no master nor doctors"). **Within this text, there is no route out
to a clinician.** A search shows only what it indexes, so the claim is scoped to this file.

What the book warns about **instead** is named, and it is not nothing — three instances, two of them from the book's own front-matter chapter summaries.
**The institution's poison:** those summaries say it — "Takes a stark look at how **the
apocalyptic theology of the Desert Fathers became the poison of Christianity**", and "Stands as a reminder to
trust your own path and not to fall for **the poison of orthodoxy**."[^poison] The second is later spelled out
as a rule about universality, not doctrine: the danger "begins when we take what worked for one person and
that person's unique conditions and turn it into a recipe for everyone".[^desert] **A named misperception:**
of Evagrius Ponticus the contents speak of "**the gnostic misperception of the mystic's path as an eternal
state of war**"[^poison] — inner conflict read as a permanent war, framed as an error rather than a state to be
managed. **A case of harm, printed in full:** the book relates what the anonymous author of its source did
with the experience, and does not tidy it — "**an autobiographical account of a dangerous state of boundless
melancholia triggered by contact with the Olympic spirits**."[^mel] And its discernment criterion states the
fifth safeguard this blog recorded, as its own rule: "**The highest teaching of magic is to understand what should be
accepted from an attending spirit, and what should be rejected**."[^arbatel]

**But the structural finding is this, and it is the sharpest in the reading.** Mapped against [the
safeguards](/safeguards/)' five, *Holy Heretics* carries three and lacks two. It has the **metered pace**,
stated as a dose; **ethics first** — "a genuine mystical path requires neither the debasement and humiliation
of others"[^feet]; and the **teacher who points past himself** — "**True empowerment is giving the student just
enough to enable them to undertake their own journey**."[^teach] What it lacks is **a plural community** and
**a discernment procedure read by someone else**. And the book says why, about its own cast:

> "All of our ancestors on this path have been **lone workers**, whether in the deserts, in their cells, or
> amongst their communities, walking their own version of the narrow path."[^thesis]

So *"we stand united as holy heretics"* is a **class, not a body**. The unity is shared experience, and a
class of isolated practitioners has no member who can read another's state — the one safeguard this blog's
model says cannot be located inside the person having the experience. **The book supplies the instrument the
frames series asked for** (a criterion, in §4) **and not the one the corpus says is required** (another
reader). That is the split, and where this reading lands.

**A counterweight, because the book is not merely silent.** It prints its sources' failures and asks the
reader to check their own projections rather than the other party's: the skills it presumes include "to
realise the Other for what it is, rather than seeing our own projections in it".[^skills] That is a **record**
offered as a mirror — and a record is not a companion. A century of other people's failures can be read alone.
It cannot read *you*.

## 7. Book II, and the rite that is published

The book's second half publishes a working ritual — a second instance of the finding made in [the operative
master](/the-operative-master/). That post's boundary sentence was "**the sources publish it**": the
operational material is in print, and the withholding there was a decision not to *republish* a technique, not
the removal of a secret. *Holy Heretics* meets that description from the other side. Its contents describe:

> "Introduces us to and provides **a full English translation of Paracelsus's little known *Arcanum
> arcanorum***, an advanced magical ritual to conjure the Olympic spirits **as well as one's holy daimon**
> through the use of magical bells."[^magic]

And its own account of what it is doing with that text is the point of interest, not an alarm. It rebuilds
the rite from fragments published separately, and says so:

> "[T]hese fragments, which four hundred years ago **were deliberately hidden by adepts in separate
> texts**", are conjoined to "present **a complete and workable version** of the Ritual of the Olympic
> Spirit. […] It will avoid **the deliberate pitfalls and traps that our ancestors set up to protect this
> secret of secrets**, and yet remain faithful and authentic to the spirit of its original sources."[^restore]

The obstacles in the older texts were **protective by design** — the book says so — and this book removes
them, in print, while stating that it is removing them. It also states, unprompted, that it does not hold
everything: the rite's starry names "will only work if passed on personally to the magician by the spirits,
and which will 'expire' after 140 years".[^restore]

**And the rite carries its own counterweights, inside the ritual text** — the opposite of what this blog
expects from a conjuration instruction, and both against over-claiming:

> "**no fantasising**. You simply shift your attention to what is already there."[^rite]

> "It is not upon you to demand anything. You are only the voice that utters the call and the spirit that
> upholds the sphere. Focus on the great presence of your mind in this sacred space, **not on any kind of
> outcome or success**."[^rite]

And one sequel detail. [The holy daimon](/the-holy-daimon/)'s counterweight was the first volume's own
metaphor — a surgeon and a camera, "the 'true will' of the camera is really the will of the doctor" — an
asymmetry that book states and does not resolve. This one's closing intoned prayer states the relation the other way:
"Through it **I behold the presence of my daimon, and my daimon beholds me**."[^rite] Beholding both ways, in
a book about what mediation costs — and closing on something small enough to report exactly: "wash your hands
with water and salt, and go to bed without speaking to anyone."[^rite]

**The counterweight.** This is a conjuration manual: not evidence that the rite works, and not a licence. What
can be reported exactly is the **form** — a method published openly, named as a reconstruction, with the
older text's designed concealment disclosed, and an explicit refusal to demand an outcome. A point of
interest, not of alarm: **a genre the blog has met only as a manual of influence acting in the other
direction.**

## 8. What the rest of the blog owes

Four corrections.

**First, [the Western column](/the-western-column/) needs a sharper subject.** Its §4 is the one headed "The
safeguards, written by practitioners for practitioners", and its finding is that the Western material is the
most useful of the three bodies for that reason. *Holy Heretics* sharpens who those practitioners were: **they
were heretics against their own institution.** Symeon's hymns were "the work of an apostate"; the Desert
Fathers' own theology is the "poison of Christianity"; the tradition the book recommends is "not kept,
confined or curated by any kind of manmade orthodoxy". **The Western column is not the Church and not the
order**, so the West's own move — to write the safeguards **into the training** — is true of Quareia and the Golden Dawn, and **not** of the material
*Holy Heretics* assembles.

**Second, the row about a curriculum gets a third shape.** [The Western column](/the-western-column/)'s "a
curriculum is a safeguard" was already qualified by [the holy daimon](/the-holy-daimon/), which is not a
curriculum. *Holy Heretics* is a book with a **prerequisite** — BOOK I gates BOOK II by instruction, and
nothing enforces it but the reader. The corpus therefore holds the row three ways: a graded syllabus
(enforced), a book with no gate, and a book with a stated gate and none. The requirement survives all three;
the **guarantee** accompanies it in one.

**Third, [the cosmos that refuses](/the-cosmos-that-refuses/) has found its practice, and should say so.**
That post concluded that the refusal is "necessary and not sufficient", that "No property of a cosmology is
self-enforcing", that "the safeguard lives in the practice rather than in any cosmology". *Holy Heretics* is that
practice at book length, answering the sharpest objection — the claim to have entered the unnamed place — with
a criterion instead of a warning. The conclusion is **supported and bounded in one reading**: the practice
exists, with an internal criterion, and it still leaves the practitioner the only reader of their own state.

**Fourth, [the operative master](/the-operative-master/) gets an instance that does the opposite of what the
post predicted.** That post could not republish a technique because it is useful to an operator and useless to
the target. Here a practitioner republishes a *rite* — and the asymmetry holds, because a rite is not gauging:
it hands over a procedure for the practitioner's own ascent, not a method of reading a person and finding the
vacancy. **That is the line the corpus can now draw**, cleaner than "the sources publish it": publishable is
the operation addressed to the person doing it, not the appraisal aimed at someone who has not asked.

---

## 9. What a reading is for

A reading holds one source **whole** — its thesis, its structure, its exercises, its omissions — and asks what
a blog that has quoted sources for years now has to change. What this one says is a split finding, and the
split is the honest result.

**This is the strongest practice-side object the corpus has met** — the apophatic refusal turned into a
metered discipline, with a criterion rather than a mood, and three of the five safeguards stated in its own
voice. **And it is missing precisely the safeguards that require a second person**, which it does not conceal
but states. So: **a practice can supply its own criterion, and a criterion is still not a second reader.**
