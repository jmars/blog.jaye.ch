# The Ladder of Light

### Enlightenment as rank — an authority nobody can check, and the reason wanting it becomes obedience

*Third of the case studies. [The method post](/the-environment/) sets out the rule; [the witch and
the debt](/the-witch-and-the-debt/) took up hidden agency. This one takes up the **second lever**:
a ranked inner state. It leans on [the sacred science](/sacred-science/) for what a frame does with
the endpoint, and on [the empty leader](/empty-leader/) for who needs the ranking.*

*Status.* The critique is documented and named, and several of the figures in it are on the record
about themselves. The description of Brazil's hierarchy as "high gain" is an **argument**, offered
as a reading; Brazil has no monopoly on ranked spirituality, and this post says so. No living
teacher is named. As everywhere in this series: this is about a structure, not a people.

---

## 1. The thing you can hear in one question

There is a question that circulates in spiritual settings and that sounds like ordinary curiosity:

> *"How evolved are you?"* — or, in the Portuguese that makes it sound gentler, *"ele é muito
> evoluído."*

It is not ordinary curiosity. It presupposes something enormous: that **inner development is a
rank**, that people stand at different heights on it, and that the heights are comparable between
persons. Consider what it would mean for that presupposition to be true — and then consider how
easily the sentence is said, and answered, as though it were nothing.

That ease is the lever. And it was described, with a name, before most of the movements that use it
had been founded.

---

## 2. The name: spiritual materialism, and the hierarchy of state

**Chögyam Trungpa** put the paradox plainly in 1973:

> "Ego is able to convert everything to its own use, even spirituality."[^trungpa]

His account of how: spiritual achievements get **collected** — knowledge, experiences, hours,
attainments — and displayed, because the display is what reassures the self that it exists, and is
real, and is *safe and secure, as a spiritual person*. The path is pursued as self-improvement by a
self that is the very thing the path is supposed to dissolve.

Fifty years later the structure got its sharpest statement, in a form that is almost a diagram:

> "Spiritual communities like these are built primarily around a hierarchy of 'subjective state.'
> The leader is taken to be one of higher subjective state than other members... social pressure
> arises to present oneself as being in as high states as possible."[^freinacht]

The author of that passage calls the error by its name — **the fallacy of turning subjective states
into social hierarchies** — and then does the thing this series respects: he lists the cases,
including figures with genuine claims to insight, and **including Trungpa himself**. That is the
tell that this is not an attack on a few bad actors. It is an account of a **structural feature** of
communities built on inner attainments.[^wilber]

And note the second-order effect he names, because a case study needs it: when the hierarchy is
built on something people cannot verify even in themselves, the social pressure does not produce
honesty. It produces **performance** — people "subtly lie to themselves and to one another" about
how lightly and profoundly they experience the world. The "strange hysterical happiness" of sect
life is not a symptom of the doctrine. It is a symptom of the **ranking**.

---

## 3. The criterion: what makes a hierarchy defensible, and what does not

Here is the distinction that turns this from an observation into an instrument — and it is the whole
of the post:

| the hierarchy is built on... | can it be checked? | is it defensible? |
|---|---|---|
| a **skill** — surgery, chess, a sport | yes, intersubjectively | yes; that is what standards are |
| a **private state** — realisation, depth, evolution | no, not by anyone, not even its owner | **no**; there is nothing to check |

A ranking over something falsifiable is not a problem. It is how competence is recognised. A ranking
over something **unfalsifiable** is a different object entirely: it is a **social position assigned
to an unobservable quantity.**[^capital] And an unobservable quantity cannot be audited — which means whoever
assigns it holds a power that no evidence can touch.

This is the same instrument as the unfalsifiable slot of the previous post, pointed not at *harm*
but at **worth**. And it produces the same asymmetry: the claim cannot be disproved, so it cannot be
refused. You cannot show that the person above you is not further along. You can only be *told*.

---

## 4. The top rung is empty, and it seals itself

Rank on an unobservable scale is already unfalsifiable. Now notice what occupies the top.

The highest-ranking claim in the genre is **the claim to have left ranking behind** — to be beyond
ego, beyond seeking, no longer moved by status. Consider the instrument it gives the person making
it: any denial confirms it (*wanting to know is the seeking*), any doubt is disproof of the
doubter's own position, and any defence of it is refutation (*he doesn't defend his reputation;
that's the mark of attainment*).

> The winning move is to claim you are not playing.

The effect is to make the top of the ladder **unassailable precisely to the degree that it is
empty.** And it produces the thing the empty-leader post described from the other end: a person
whose standing is made of everyone else's projection, with nothing underneath to check it. The
ranking is not something such a leader *uses*. In the model's terms it is closer to the reverse —
his self is **externalised into it**, and the height of the ladder is the measure of what must be
believed about him.

---

## 5. Why this is a predation lever: status-seeking *is* obedience

Now the reason this belongs in this series rather than in a contemplation essay.

In a normal hierarchy, climbing costs you **work** — you get better at something checkable, and the
rung you reach is the rung you earned. In a hierarchy of private state, the rungs are **granted**.
There is no work that is not judgment by the same authority the rungs belong to. Which means:

- **The seeker must submit to climb.** You cannot earn a state you cannot measure; you can only be
  recognised as having it. Recognition comes from above.
- **The authority is the ceiling.** Whatever he says about your position is your position, because
  there is no other measurement.
- **Progress is unfalsifiable, so it is endless.** There is always a deeper layer, a subtler
  resistance, further still to go — and the one who tells you where you are is the one you keep
  needing.

So the ladder is not a side-effect of the group; it is a **control surface**. And it recruits the
person's own ambition: the desire for the next rung does the work that a rule or a threat would
otherwise have to do. The predation is not done *to* the seeker's want. It is done **through** it.[^abuse]

---

## 6. Brazil: the ladder at high gain

Here the reading is thinner, and it is marked as a reading.

An environment supplies the ladder more or less readily. Where authority is informal and flat, "how
evolved are you" has less to attach to. Where a society runs on **hierarchy and personal
standing** — a strongly status-conscious order, with the personal authority of a name, a title, a
lineage, and the ordinary weight of *"você sabe com quem está falando"* — the ladder has more to
grip.

Brazil's religious field makes this concrete rather than abstract. The **grades and lines** are not
imported ornaments: Umbanda and Candomblé run on **initiatory degrees and obligations**; Santo Daime
and the União do Vegetal organise on **hierarchies of function and grade**; Conscienciologia writes
the ladder down most explicitly of all, grading consciousness from the least developed to the
**serenão** at the top.[^brazil] This is not a culture that needs to have rank explained to it. Rank is the
ambient grammar.

Which is the lever: a predator does not have to install a hierarchy. He has to **occupy the top of
one.** And a culture that already reads inner state as a scale has the ladder standing before he
arrives.

The counterweight belongs in the same breath, for the same reason as always. Hierarchies in these
traditions are also **how knowledge is transmitted safely** — a graded path, a named teacher with
accountability to a lineage, an initiation that makes obligations mutual. The lever is not the
existence of rank. It is **rank on an unfalsifiable quantity, held by an unaccountable person.**

---

## 7. The countermeasure, and where it is older than the problem

Two traditions already fenced this, and both are worth returning to the reader.

**One refused the claim.** In the Buddhist monastic code, it is a *pārājika* — a defeat, expulsion —
to claim a superhuman attainment one does not have. The reasoning is stated as bluntly as any
regulator would want:

> "This is the foremost great thief: he who falsely claims to have attained a superhuman state which
> he has not attained... you have eaten the country's almsfood by theft."[^parajika]

The rule is careful in exactly the way this series tries to be careful: it targets **fraud**, and
explicitly exempts sincere **overestimation** — the practitioner who genuinely but wrongly believes
he has attained something is not the thief. Keep that distinction in view, because the predatory
case inverts it: it is the *deliberate* claim, dressed as sincerity, that the rule names. The
institution that claims to rank attainment is also the institution that made the claim a crime.

**The other refused the endpoint.** As [the sacred science](/sacred-science/) argued, the traditions
with the longest record are apophatic — *neti neti*, "not this, not that" — and their refusal to
name the destination is a **safety mechanism**: a collapse aimed at a named end can be owned, and a
teacher can stand at the end he supplied. The same logic applies one level down. A teacher who
**points past himself** cannot be the ladder's top; a tradition that refuses to say where the path
ends cannot rank you against your neighbour.

That is the whole of the post, and it is the same shape as the rest of this series. The lever is an
**unfalsifiable authority**. The countermeasure is not a better authority. It is a **refusal to
provide one**.

[^capital]: On spiritual mastery as a positional good within a competitive religious field — Verter,
B. (2003), "Spiritual Capital: Theorizing Religion with Bourdieu against Bourdieu," *Sociological
Theory* 21(2): 150–174, https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9558.00182
(pdf: https://users.drew.edu/omaduro/bourdieu/pdf/VerterSpiritualCapital.pdf) — religious knowledge,
competencies and preferences treated as "positional goods within a competitive symbolic economy."
Verter's point is that such capital is real and analysable; this post's use of it is narrower — that
where the good being competed for is unobservable, the competition can only be adjudicated by an
authority.

[^parajika]: *Pārājika* 4 (Vinaya) — wrongfully claiming noble attainments; the "foremost great
thief" passage; and the exemption for claims made through *adhimāna* (sincere overestimation).

[^abuse]: On the structural potential for abuse in the master–disciple relationship — Puttick, E.
(1995), "Sexuality, Gender and the Abuse of Power in the Master–Disciple Relationship: The Case of
the Rajneesh Movement," *Journal of Contemporary Religion* 10(1): 29–40,
https://doi.org/10.1080/13537909508580724 — which treats the abuse not as the defect of one teacher
but as a property of the relationship between an unaccountable master and a disciple who has vested
their standing in his judgement. The frame it describes is the one this post calls the ladder; the
term "control surface" is this post's.

[^brazil]: On initiatory grades and hierarchy in the Brazilian religions named:
**Umbanda / Candomblé** — Camargo, A. F. G. de, & Scorsolini-Comin, F. (2018), "A feitura do santo:
percursos desenvolvimentais de médiuns em iniciação no Candomblé," *Psicologia & Sociedade* 30:
e189741, https://www.scielo.br/j/psoc/a/yv6f6CVNJSYmxnXDbsLcQ7G — documents the graded sequence *abiã* →
*iaô* / *ogan* / *ekedi* → *ebômi* (at seven years and the "obrigações" of the 1st, 3rd, 5th and 7th

**Santo Daime** — Assis, G. L. de (2013), *Encanto e desencanto: um estudo sociológico sobre a
inserção do Santo Daime no cenário religioso contemporâneo* (master's dissertation, UFMG),
https://repositorio.ufmg.br/bitstreams/de2d2a01-e278-449a-8fde-0d9c405e54c7/download — the *fardado*

**União do Vegetal** — Melo, R. V. (2013), "Encantamento e disciplina na União do Vegetal," *Anuário

into the *corpo instrutivo* and the hierarchy of levels above it.

this post's argument, not a measured claim.
