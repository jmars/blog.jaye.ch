# The Deep Inheritance

### Four frames, three columns, and one inheritance older than writing — the convergence, at the scale this series works at

*Sixth and last of the frames — the series' closer, and the one that makes the case for the whole of it.
[The traditions post](/what-the-traditions-knew/) found seven contemplative traditions naming the same
failure without contact. [The Western column](/the-western-column/) found a third. This post is about
the **fourth** — and about the timescale that makes the other three look recent.*

*Status.* The material below is quoted from published work and cited; the reading of it through this
series' model is an **inference**, offered as one. **The post is not an argument about prehistory** —
§6 says exactly what it does and does not claim — and it does not adjudicate any cosmology. No living
author is diagnosed.

---

## 1. What this series has been doing, said once, at the end

The `frames` series began with a claim and ends by testing it against a timescale. The claim was: **a
cosmology is a frame, and every frame can be checked — or not — for what it names and who benefits from
the naming.** Five posts ran that on the frames the West is currently caught between:

- **the machine frame** — checks, cannot read; leaves a **vacancy**;
- **a cosmos of persons** — reads, cannot check; safeguard is practice, not ontology;
- **the owned cosmos** — reads, has a purpose, and has a **seat**; the form every case study shares;
- **the cosmos that refuses** — a correspondence with no endpoint; the architectural countermeasure,
  and not self-enforcing.

And the finding was that **no frame is safe by virtue of what it says**. Every structural safeguard can
be counterfeited, and what decides is a **practice**.

Now this post puts the same question to a frame far older than any of those — the cosmology that human
beings carried out of Africa, and the one they built afterwards — because if the series' claims are
about *structures* rather than about modernity, they should show up in the oldest material anyone has.

---

## 2. The claim: mythology is inherited, and inherited deeply

The argument comes from **Michael Witzel's** *The Origins of the World's Mythologies* (Oxford, 2012),
and Gordon White's *Star.Ships* is built on it. The finding is a **phylogeny** of myth — the same method
by which historical linguists reconstruct a lost language from its descendants — and it sorts the
world's mythologies into two deep families with a shared root.

**Gondwana mythology — the older layer, dated in White's summary to roughly 65,000 years:**

> "This is the cosmology our ancestors took with them when they left Africa. These are described as 'a
> **forest of stories**,' which is a collection of discrete tales pertaining to the origin of individual
> creatures, how mankind first appeared, and taboos around tribal morality. **Gondwana mythologies
> display little interest in the creation or destruction of the universe; indeed, probably the defining
> characteristic of a Gondwana mythology is that the creation/destruction story is absent and the
> universe has always existed and will always exist.**"[^starships]

And where it survives: "Sub-Saharan Africa, Australia, New Guinea and parts of the western Pacific, all
places of **long-term isolation** from other cultures."[^starships]

**Laurasian mythology — the later layer, roughly 40,000 years:**

> "Sometime around this date, somewhere in Asia or Eurasia, mankind developed what Witzel calls '**our
> first novel**.' Laurasian mythologies **begin with the creation of the world/universe and end with its
> destruction.** In the intervening time we find the separation of heaven and earth, Creation existing
> through repeated epochs, **the killing of a dragon or monster, a Golden Age and a Flood as punishment
> for mankind**."[^starships]

Which reaches, in White's account, "Indo-Aryan mythology (and thus Sumerian, Babylonian and Vedic)" —
and, through the Eurasian dispersal, the Americas.

**Two shapes, then.** The older one has **no cosmogony and no eschatology**: the world is simply there,
and what matters is the local and the moral. The later one has **both, and a story between them** — a
beginning, a fall, a catastrophe, a renewal. And the dating is the argument: a common pool of motifs
across populations that had been separated for tens of millennia cannot have been passed by contact
afterwards. It was **inherited.**

---

## 3. Why that is the same finding as the rest of the series

Here is the shape of it, and it is worth stating plainly because it is the point of the whole post.

The traditions post found **seven contemplative traditions, with no contact between them,[^traditions]** naming the
same failure and prescribing the same five safeguards. The Western column found a **third** body of
practice arriving at the same list. The clinic found the same requirements from case data, calling it
harm-monitoring. Three independent convergences — and the question each time was: *is this a cultural
transmission, or a structure?*

Witzel's finding is the **fourth convergence, one scale down and one timescale up.** It is not a
convergence of *responses* to the collapse. It is a convergence of **cosmologies** — the frames
themselves, at the deepest layer anyone can reconstruct — and the conclusion is the one the series has
been reaching at every level:

> **The shape of a cosmology is inherited, not invented.** Long before any tradition wrote a safeguard
> down, the frame that a person would need a safeguard inside of was already in place, tens of millennia
> old, and being handed on.

That matters to this series because it relocates the whole argument. The blog has been talking about
cosmologies people *choose* — the machine frame, the living cosmos, the owned cosmos. Witzel's finding
is that behind the chosen frames is an **inherited** one, and it is not chosen at all. Which is exactly
[the opener](/the-frame-is-a-variable/)'s point,[^frames] made at the largest scale available: **a frame you did
not choose is the frame hardest to see.**

---

## 4. And the inherited frame contains the question

Now the structural detail, because it is where this post earns its place in the series.

Look again at the two shapes. The **older** layer has no cosmogony and no eschatology — no creation, no
destruction — and White is explicit that this is "probably the defining characteristic." The **later**
layer has both, and between them the story that everyone raised in a literate culture will recognise:
a separation, epochs, a dragon, a golden age, a flood.

**Which means the two oldest frames on record disagree about exactly the thing this series has been
about.** The Gondwanan shape says: *the universe has always existed, and always will — there is no
endpoint.* The Laurasian shape says: *it began, it will end, and there is a story in between* — which
is a cosmology **with a telos**, and a telos is the precondition for a seat.

That is not a coincidence of vocabulary. It is the structural distinction of posts
[three](/a-cosmos-of-persons/) and [four](/the-owned-cosmos/) — endpoint or no endpoint — appearing at
the root of the human record, in the two layers of the oldest story we have. **The families are older
and younger, but the distinction is not a sequence** — White, working from Witzel, is explicit that the
categorisation "is not an evolutionary one": both collections of stories continue to the present day,
and the defining difference he draws is not chronology but emphasis, "the power of the word" in the
Laurasian material against the Gondwanan stress on physical objects.[^notsequence] What the
*specialisation* means is that a frame **with** a telos is not the primitive form the later world grew
out of — it is the younger of two living forms, and one of them had the endpoint and one did not from
the beginning the record reaches. This post does not lean on the chronology; the structure is the claim.

**Which does not make the later frame worse.** It is the frame that produced almost everything this
blog cites: the Vedic, Babylonian, Greek, Abrahamic and Hermetic traditions are all Laurasian, and
[sacred-science](/sacred-science/)'s apophatic refusal — the strongest countermeasure in the corpus —
grew **inside** the frame that names an endpoint. The finding is narrower and better: **a frame with an
endpoint can hold the refusal**, and one of the oldest traditions did exactly that. The safeguard is
not the shape. It never was.

## 5. The counterweight, which at this scale is a warning

The counterweight here has to be unusually serious, because prehistory is the one subject in this series
where a reader's judgement is easiest to capture and hardest to check.

**The dates are model-dependent, and the experts disagree.** White gives Gondwanan mythology as
"65,000 years old" and Laurasian as "40,000" — and those figures are **reconstructions from a
phylogenetic method**, not dates any archaeologist excavated. Witzel's own account is careful about
this; White himself notes that "pinpointing the best case for precisely where in Asia requires the use
of data points that Dr Witzel **studiously avoids**."[^starships] A method that reconstructs a tree from
surviving motifs can be right about the **relationships** and wrong about the **clock**, and the two
errors are independent.

**And the corpus's own examples show the trap.** The same book mounts an argument about the Younger
Dryas and the flood stories — a hypothesis in genuine scientific dispute, with published rebuttals, and
White lists one in his own bibliography ("Comet theory false; doesn't explain cold snap at the end of
the Ice Age")[^starships]. Which is to say: *Star.Ships* is careful and it is also a **thesis with a
side**, and a post in this series has to say so rather than borrowing its authority.

**So this post does not lean on the chronology.** Its claim is about the **structure** — two inherited
shapes, one without a telos and one with — and that claim survives whether the split is 40,000 years
old or 15,000, and whether the comet was involved or not. If the dates fall, the post's argument is
untouched. That is the right place to put a load-bearing claim when the evidence is contested.

---

## 6. The boundary, stated exactly

Four things, and the second is the one this subject demands.

**The post does not argue about prehistory.** Whether myths were inherited from an out-of-Africa
population, when they diverged, and what caused the divergence: those are questions for the people who
work on them, and this series is not competent to adjudicate them. It takes the **structural**
distinction — telos or no telos — which is visible in the material whatever the dating.

**The post does not adopt a cosmology, and this is not an argument for "ancient wisdom."** The finding
is not that the old frames were right. It is that **frames are inherited**, which is a claim about how
human beings come by their cosmologies, not about which one is true. A reader who takes this as *the
ancients knew* has made the same error [the opener](/the-frame-is-a-variable/) warned against, at a
grander scale.

**And it does not license primitivism in either direction.** The Laurasian frame — with its telos, its
Flood, its fall — is not a corruption, and the Gondwanan one is not a purity. The series has been
careful about this all the way through: [the machine frame](/the-machine-has-no-reading/) is not the
villain, [the living cosmos](/a-cosmos-of-persons/) is not the hero, [mediation](/the-owned-cosmos/) is
not automatically an abuse, and [the refusal](/the-cosmos-that-refuses/) is not the solution. The
inherited layers are frames. They get the same treatment.

**No one is diagnosed**, and in this post the temptation is to diagnose a *culture* — to say the
Laurasian frame is the one that made seats possible, or the Gondwanan one is the one that kept people
free. That is [the label post](/the-label/)'s error at the scale of a civilisation,[^label] and it is refused:
the post describes two shapes and makes no verdict about the people who held them.

---

## 7. What the post claims, and what the series ends with

**One.** The frames the series has been examining sit on top of an **inherited** one, tens of millennia
old, and the inheritance is detectable: two deep families, one without a telos and one with, sharing a
root.

**Two.** The telos is a remembered and ancient thing, not a modern invention — and, additively, it
appears to be a **later** layer than the no-endpoint shape. Which means the two arrangements this
series has been distinguishing all along are not marginal: they are the oldest recorded disagreement
about what reality is.

**Three.** And the series' conclusion is now complete, at the largest scale it can reach. Four frames
examined, three columns of convergence, one deep inheritance — and **the same finding at every level**:

> **No cosmology is safe. No frame is self-enforcing. The safeguard is a practice, and the practice is
> the same wherever it is written down: keep the endpoint unnamed, keep the route plural, keep a check
> that does not benefit from the answer, and keep the pace metered.**

That is what the series set out to test, and it survived the test — not because the frames are
equivalent, but because the thing that makes a frame safe **was never in the frame.** It is in what a
community does with it, and it has to be done again every generation, which is — and this is the last
thing worth saying — **exactly why the oldest material is worth reading.** Two and a half thousand years
of notes about this territory exist, and they were written because the practice keeps being lost.

The blog's contribution is not a cosmology, and it is not a warning about anyone's beliefs. It is a
**test** — and now, at the end, the test's own shape is visible in the record it was derived from:

> **Ask what the frame names. Ask whether it can be checked by someone who does not benefit. Ask where
> the seat is.** The answers do not tell you what is true. They tell you what to look at — and that is
> the whole of the discipline, and the reason this series has four frames, three columns, and no
> doctrine to sell.
