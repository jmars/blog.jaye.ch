# A Cosmos of Persons

### The frame that has a reading for the collapse — and the two structural properties that keep it from owning it

*Third of the frames. [The opener](/the-frame-is-a-variable/) found the West caught between two frames;
[the previous post](/the-machine-has-no-reading/) took the machine frame apart and found its blind spot
— no category for the interior, and therefore a **vacancy** the collapse falls into. This post takes the
other frame: the one that has a reading.*

*Status.* The ethnographic and theoretical material below is quoted from published work and cited; the
reading of it through this series' model is an **inference**, offered as one. The post is about **what a
frame can do**, not about whether the cosmos is inhabited — §6 states why that distinction is the whole
of it. No cosmology is endorsed; no living author is diagnosed; and the frame is not treated as a
solution, for reasons §5 gives.

---

## 1. The frame on the other side of the vacancy

The previous post ended on a specific claim.[^prev] **A frame with no reading for the collapse is not neutral,
it is a vacancy**, and a vacancy gets filled by whoever arrives first. The machine frame leaves exactly
that — an experience of dissolution with no category, and therefore no language, and therefore nobody
speaking.

There is a frame that does not leave it. If the cosmos is made of persons rather than of dead matter,
then the collapse is not a malfunction and not a trick; the frame has a **third reading** available, and
that reading is *something was met*.

That sounds like a resolution, and the whole point of this post is that it is not one. A frame with a
reading is **more dangerous** than a frame without one, in a specific and identifiable way — because
a frame that has a reading is a frame that can **own** it. So the question this post asks is not *does
this frame have a reading* (it does) but:

> **What keeps a frame with a reading from owning it?**

And the answer turns out to be structural rather than sentimental, and it is the same answer the
traditions gave — arrived at from a completely different direction.

---

## 2. What the frame says the cosmos is made of

Take the description first, from White's account of Pohnpei, drawing on its ethnography — and read it as
a **frame**, in the way the previous post read materialist-naturalism, without adopting it.

> "It is noteworthy that there is little or no distinction between the human and the non-human.
> **Everything is made of the same 'stuff' which is a kind of personhood**… Removing the 'separateness'
> of humans, or rather returning humans to the same state of personhood as everything else, has the
> flow on effect of **leaving no room for the notion of 'nature.'** Nature is constituted as something
> separate from humans, but if there is no separateness, there is no nature."[^animystic]

Follow the consequence, because it is the exact mirror of the last post's. Where materialist-naturalism
gives you **dead matter** and therefore no interior, this frame gives you **personhood everywhere** and
therefore no exterior. And the boundary that disappears is not one that anybody imposed: *"nature"* was
constituted by separating humans out, so if nothing is separated out, there is nothing to be the
"natural" as opposed to the "supernatural," and nothing for a *culture* to be a thing *about*.

Anthropology has a name for what the frame does to agency as well, and Tim Ingold gives it in a line
that is worth keeping — it is a description of **custodianship** rather than domination:

> "Human beings do not so much transform the material world as **play their part, along with other
> creatures, in the world's transformation of itself.**"[^animystic]

And the felt consequence, in White's own summary, is the one that matters for a practitioner:

> "when you step into it, **your universe suddenly gets a lot more crowded.**"[^animystic]

Crowded is the operative word. That is not a mood; it is a **structural** claim about the frame: there
are more participants than there were. Which is where the safeguard comes from.

---

## 3. Plurality, which is the safeguard

Here is the whole argument of this post in one move, and it is a move this series has made before.

[The safeguards](/safeguards/) found that the traditions encode **plural community** as one of the five
requirements — the community around a practitioner must not be a single voice, and must not be only the
group. And [the coercion post](/manufacturing-the-collapse/) found the inversion: a coercive group
supplies *exclusivity* where the tradition supplies plurality. The group becomes the only available
reading, the only available relationship, the only hand within reach.[^safeguards]

Now look at what a cosmos of persons does structurally. **A cosmos in which everything is a participant
cannot have a single endpoint** — not by moral preference, but by construction. The moss, the pig, the
compost, the dead, the weather, the ancestors: each is a party. There is no position in such a cosmos
that is *the* place where meaning is dispensed, because meaning is being worked out **among**
participants, continuously, and no one of them is the whole.

That is the safeguard, and it is **architectural** rather than virtuous. It is the same property
[sacred-science](/sacred-science/) identified in the apophatic traditions — the refusal to name the
endpoint — arriving from cosmology instead of from doctrine. Both are ways of removing the steering
wheel. And it is why this frame, whatever else is true of it, **cannot easily be run as a cult
cosmology**: a cult needs a mediator, and this frame's structure has no seat for one.

---

## 4. And unowned, which is the harder half

Plurality is the first property. The second is the one a modern practitioner is most likely to fail,
and it is the one White is sharpest about.

The frame can be held as **apparatus**. He reserves his criticism not for sceptics but for practitioners
who treat their own tradition's regimen as optional costume — who have

> "dismissed the sequence of prayers and divine names as 'too Christian' for their modern tastes or some
> sort of **optional ambiance** to somehow 'get into the ritual mood.'"[^animystic]

This is worth pausing on, because it is the frame's own failure mode and it is not exotic. **To treat
the regimen as ambiance is to place yourself above it** — to decide what the practice "really" means,
and to select the parts that suit you. Which is precisely the move this series has documented in a guru:
the one who supplies the reading, from outside the thing being read.

So the two properties are related, and together they are the safeguard:

> **Plural**, so no single participant is the endpoint. **Unowned**, so no single practitioner is
> either.

A cosmos of persons in which *you* decide which persons count, and on what terms, is not plural and not
unowned. It is a machine frame with the decorations left on — the practitioner as the only real
subject, and everything else as setting.

---

## 5. The honest turn: a cosmos of persons is not a safe frame

Now the part the post has to say, because a reader could have taken the last three sections as a
recommendation, and this series does not make those.

**A cosmos of persons includes harmful persons.** This is not a footnote. Every case study in this
series is, in its own idiom, a cosmos of persons — the container, the archons, the entities
that interfere, the spirits that demand.[^mechanism] **The frames that predate predation are themselves animist
frames**, and the predation happens *inside* them. "Everything is a person" is not a safeguard; it is a
description of the **plane** on which the contest takes place.

What distinguishes the safe version from the unsafe one is therefore not the ontology. It is the two
properties of §3 and §4, and they are **properties of practice, not of metaphysics**:

- a plane with **many** participants, so that no one of them is the whole;
- a practitioner who does not **own** the reading — who is answerable to the participants, rather than
  managing them.

Which is why the frame is not a solution, and why the series will not recommend it: **the ontology
does not deliver the safeguard. The practice does.** A cosmos of persons can be run as a coercive
cosmology, and there is nothing in the ontology that forbids it — only in the discipline.

And there is a harder version of this, from a contemporary writer White cites, that cuts even deeper
and is worth taking exactly as it is:

> "**What if the way we respond to the crisis is part of the crisis?**"[^animystic]

That question — Bayo Akomolafe's, quoted in *Ani.Mystic* — is the apophatic refusal at the level of
**action**. It declines to supply the response, on the grounds that the supplied response may be the
problem. Read it against this series and it is [the label post](/the-label/)'s discipline in another
register: *describe, ask, do not diagnose.* Do not supply the person's reading for them, **including
the reading that would fix them.**

And note the shape of it, because it is the same shape as §3. **If even your response is under
suspicion, then you have removed the steering wheel from your own intervention.** That is the discipline
of a frame that will not name the endpoint — now applied to the person holding the frame.

---

## 6. The boundary, and the gap this post has to admit

Four things, and the third is a real gap rather than a hedge.

**First: this post does not claim the cosmos is inhabited.** Whether there are persons everywhere, or
matter is dead, is a question about the world, and this series does not adjudicate those — it said so in
the opener and it holds. The post's claims are about **frames**: what this one can do, what it cannot,
and what structure keeps it from being owned.

**Second: it does not claim the frame is safe.** §5 is the operative section. The ontology does not
carry the safeguard.

**Third — and this is the honest gap: this frame lacks the machine frame's one great virtue.** The
machine frame is **checkable**. A cosmos of impersonal forces makes claims that anyone can test, and
[the previous post](/the-machine-has-no-reading/) argued that this is not a small thing — it is the
frame that made auditable measurement possible, and it is the frame that refuses unfalsifiable
authority. The living cosmos is difficult to check in that way: its claims are about participants and
relations, and a claim about what a river wants is not falsifiable by the methods the machine frame
built.

So the two frames are **complementary in their safeguards, not ranked**:

| | checkable | plural & unowned |
|---|---|---|
| **the machine frame** | yes — and this is its great virtue | no — no reading, so the vacancy |
| **the living cosmos** | not in the same way — a real weakness | yes — and this is its safeguard |

Neither is complete. And this is the post's actual finding, and it is why the series does not pick a
side: **the frame that can see the collapse cannot check itself, and the frame that can check itself
cannot see the collapse.** A reader who wants the series to endorse one has not read the two columns
of that table.

**Fourth: no one is diagnosed**, and in this post that includes the modern practitioner of §4 — who is
a **position** (anyone who takes up a tradition's regimen while standing above it), not a type, and not
an accusation against any particular person or lineage.

---

## 7. What the post claims

Three claims.

**One.** A cosmology with a reading for the collapse is more dangerous than one without, not less,
because a reading can be **owned** — and the danger is not in the reading but in the ownership.

**Two.** What prevents ownership is **structural and not sentimental**: a plane of many participants,
and a practitioner who does not stand above the reading. Those two properties are the same safeguard
[the traditions](/what-the-traditions-knew/) encoded as plural community and the apophatic refusal,
arrived at here from cosmology.

**Three.** No frame has both safeguards. The machine frame checks and cannot see; this one sees and
cannot check. **The series will consequently recommend no cosmology** — and that is not a failure of
nerve but the finding: the safeguards that matter in this series have never been properties of a
metaphysics. They are properties of a **practice**, which is why they travel across frames as different
as a Buddhist meditation hall, a clinical instrument, and an ethnography of a Micronesian island.

Which leaves the series' fourth post, and it is the dark one. The machine frame leaves a vacancy. The
living cosmos can be owned. And there is a third possibility, and it is what happens when a frame
**names the endpoint and stations someone at it** — not a blind spot and not a safeguard, but an
occupied one. That is [the sacred science](/sacred-science/) at the scale of a world, and it is next.
