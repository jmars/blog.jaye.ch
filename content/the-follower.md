# The Follower

### The follower is a position, not a personality — and the instrument that would sort people is the one that fails

*A companion to [the empty leader](/empty-leader/) — the same relation, seen from the other end.
That post
found the leader's mind is the shape he reproduces. This one asks about the other end of the
relation — and about a literature that spent seventy years trying to name the follower as a **type**,
and ended up dissolving the type instead.*

*Status.* The psychometric findings, the scale critiques and the model comparisons below are
measured and published; the placing of them in this series' framework is an **inference**, offered as
one. It names no living person, diagnoses no population, and takes no political side — which, as §6
shows, is the whole difficulty, because the literature it draws on has *not* taken no side, and the
series has to say so.

---

## 1. The question the empty leader left open

[The empty leader](/empty-leader/) argued that the leader's self is externalized — made of followers —
and that the group is structurally his `G`, his generator of self-content. Which raises the obvious
question the post did not answer:

> **What is a follower?**

The intuition, and the one the whole culture reaches for, is that the follower is a **kind of person**:
a type, a disposition, something you *are*. The research literature agrees, superficially — it even
named the type. And then, over seventy years, it took the type apart.

That is the finding this post is about, and it is unusually useful, because the takedown is more
interesting than the type ever was.

---

## 2. The literature that named the type

The tradition starts in 1950 with Adorno and colleagues' *The Authoritarian Personality*, which
proposed an authoritarian character structure to explain the rise of fascism. It was enormously
influential — and, by the account of the field itself, it was also **criticised at the time for bias
and methodology**, its theory falling "into disfavor" partly through its association with Freudian
psychoanalysis.[^adorno]

What matters here is not that it was wrong. It is **how** it failed, because the failure is one this
series has met four times already.

The instrument was the **F-scale** — "F" for pre-fascist personality. Its items were written in
"superficially moderate language" that nonetheless conveyed the target, and they were **all phrased in
the same direction**. The check for that is to reverse each item and see whether the scale still
measures the same thing. It did not: the correlation between the F-scale and an item-by-item reversed
version of itself was **.20** — where, if phrasing played no role, it should have been **−1.00**.[^adorno]

Sit with that number. The scale's correlation with its own negation is **positive**, which means a
substantial part of what it measured was not the construct — it was **acquiescence**, the tendency to
agree. The instrument that would sort people into authoritarians and non-authoritarians was measuring,
in part, *the willingness to say yes to a questionnaire.*

That is the **DES-T's 54% false-positive rate** in [the mechanism post](/meditation-harm/), met again
in a different literature: **the instrument that names the person is the instrument that fails.** And
the failure has the same structure — a self-report scale, whose variance is partly the *mode* of
reporting rather than the thing reported.

---

## 3. The repair, and what it kept

The type was not abandoned; it was rebuilt, and the rebuilding is what makes the literature usable.
**Bob Altemeyer** developed the **RWA scale** ("right-wing authoritarianism") to measure the construct
rigorously, and defined it by three attitudes: a high degree of **submission** to established
authorities; **aggression** directed at targets the authorities sanction; and high **adherence to
social conventions**.[^altemeyer]

And then he did the thing that matters most for this post, and it was deliberate:

> Altemeyer calls right-wing authoritarians **"authoritarian followers"** — "*to emphasize that he is
> not speaking of authoritarian leaders*, which is the more commonly understood meaning." He reserves
> a different term for leaders: the **"social dominator."**[^altemeyer]

**The follower and the leader are not one category.** The finding is a measurement's, not a moralist's:
the literature separated them, and **Duckitt's dual-process model** gave them different engines — the
authoritarian-follower path runs on **threat**, the dominator path on **dominance**.[^duckitt]

Read that against [the empty leader](/empty-leader/). That post argued the two share one dissociation
pointing opposite ways; this literature says they are **two different types with two different
drivers**. They are not in conflict — as §5 argues, they are the same structure seen from the survey
side and the model side — but it is worth noting that the empirical field reached the *distinction*
first, and from entirely different evidence.

---

## 4. And then the type dissolved

Here is the finding that does the work, and it comes from **Karen Stenner's** *The Authoritarian
Dynamic* (2005).

Her argument is that prior work on authoritarianism suffered from **tautological assertions** and had
failed to produce a theory that explained how authoritarian expression **fluctuates with conditions**.
Her correction is a single structural move: RWA is

> "best understood as a dynamic response to external threat, **not a static disposition**."[^stenner]

And she makes the operational distinction that follows: the RWA scale measures **expressed**
authoritarianism, while a *latent predisposition* is what threatening circumstances convert into that
response.[^stenner]

Read that in this series' own vocabulary and it says something the series has been arguing from the
first post:

> **A latent disposition, plus a condition, produces the behaviour.**

Not a kind of person. A **position the conditions activate**. Which is
[the-environment](/the-environment/)'s thesis — the levers are positions an environment supplies or
withholds, not possessions of a people — and it arrives here as a survey-measured claim rather than a
model. Her cross-national evidence is the same shape: she reports authoritarian intolerance running
**low in Eastern Europe while socialist autocracies supplied normative reassurance**, and rising when
that reassurance collapsed.[^stenner] **Take away the condition and the disposition does not express.**

That is the most usable single sentence in this whole literature for this series, and it is worth
stating as a rule:

> **The environment does not create the follower. It decides whether the predisposition is expressed.**

Which is the price-and-condition argument of [the Costume](/the-costume/) and
[the Breakthrough](/the-breakthrough/), reached from political psychology by someone studying
electorates.

She also draws a line this series should keep, because it resolves a conflation that otherwise
poisons the discussion: **authoritarianism is resistance to difference across *space*; conservatism is
resistance to difference across *time*.**[^stenner] One is aversion to diversity *now*; the other is
aversion to change *over time*. They are different axes, and they are routinely merged.

---

## 5. Why the follower is a *position*: the model's account

Now put the psychology beside the model, because they agree, and the agreement is the point.

[The empty leader](/empty-leader/) established the leader's structural need. A self made of followers
needs followers **who can be supply** — which is why the group's demand for **sameness** is not
decoration: "a group of a hundred is a hundredfold larger self-content generator than one person, and
none of it is *his*." The follower is recruited, in that account, into a role — the `G`-unit.

And what the psychology literature adds is that the **role is what explains the person**, not a
trait. Three lines of evidence, all in the same direction:

- **Stenner**: expression is conditional on threat. Remove the condition, and the expression goes.
- **Altemeyer**: the follower is defined by *relation to* authority — submission to the established,
  aggression toward the sanctioned. That is a **relational** definition, not a trait definition. A
  follower without the authority is not a follower.
- **Haslam & Reicher**: tyranny is a **group-identity dynamic**, and followers are **active
  participants** in it — engaged in producing and reproducing the leader — not passive victims of a
  defect.[^haslam]

The last one matters most, because it upsets the comfortable story. The follower is not the person who
*failed* to resist. The follower is a **participant** — and the position is one that any of us can
occupy, given the conditions, because the conditions are what call it out. That is the same conclusion
the whole series reaches about the *levers*: they are positions, they are general, and the question is
never "what kind of person" but **"what does this environment supply, and to whom"**.

So the answer to §1 is:

> **A follower is someone standing in a position that a condition has activated, in relation to an
> authority that defines the position.**

Which is why the follower cannot be diagnosed and the leader can be described: the leader *needs* the
position filled, and will build the conditions; the follower is *produced by* the conditions the
leader builds. One is a design; the other is a response.

---

## 6. The part this post has to be honest about

The series' rule is to describe structures and to refuse to diagnose people — and this is the subject
where that rule is hardest to keep, so the difficulty goes in the body rather than the notes.

**The literature is not politically neutral, and this post will not pretend it is.** The dominant
construct is measured as **right-wing** authoritarianism; it was born as an attempt to explain
fascism; and it is routinely deployed in political argument as a way of diagnosing one's opponents.
Altemeyer himself noted the mirror case — a "left-wing authoritarian" who submits to authorities
seeking to *overthrow* the establishment[^altemeyer] — and Stenner explicitly notes that the dynamic
appears **elsewhere on the political spectrum**, citing the Nation of Islam as an example.[^stenner]

That matters because the honest use of this literature **dissolves** the political application rather
than arming it. If authoritarian expression is **a response to threat**, then **the way to produce it
is to produce the threat** — which means the interesting question is never *"who are the
authoritarians"* but *"who is making people feel that their world is coming apart."* The first
question is a sorting exercise that tells you about the sorter, exactly as
[the label post](/the-label/) says;[^label] the second is a question about conditions, and it has an
answer.

And the series' own rule applies to this post with unusual force, so it goes in full:
**this is not a diagnostic instrument.** Nothing here identifies anyone as a follower, nothing here
licenses treating anyone as one, and the standing limit holds — *a description of an environment is
not a finding about a person's mind.* The whole finding of §4 is that the position is **conditional**,
which means every person in it is a person in a **condition**, not a member of a kind. Anyone who
cites this to sort people into followers and leaders has inverted it, and reproduced the F-scale's
error at the level of argument.

---

## 7. Why the follower is the series' hardest case — and its clearest

Every post in this series has been about a **position**: the witch-and-the-debt's unfalsifiable slot,
the ladder's rank, the certified frame's borrowed warrant, the machine's unarguable authority. The
Follower is the same finding, one level up, about **the person in the position** — and it is the one
that closes the loop with [the empty leader](/empty-leader/).

| | the leader | the follower |
|---|---|---|
| what the model says | a self externalized into others | a self-content generator recruited as supply |
| what the psychology says | a dominator: **dominance**-driven | a follower: **threat**-driven |
| what defines the position | the need to be supplied | **relation to an authority** |
| what activates it | opportunity | **a condition** (perceived threat to the world) |
| can it be diagnosed? | no | **no** — and the instrument that tried failed at .20 |

The last row is the post's contribution, and it is what makes this the series' clearest case rather
than its most dangerous one. The tradition tried to name the follower as a type for seventy years and
could not — not because the research was lazy, but because **the thing it was looking for is not in
the person.** It is in the relation, and in the condition, and in the authority that defines both.

Which is the same conclusion every lever in this series has reached, and the reason the series never
names a type: **a position is what a person is standing in, and a person can leave one.**
