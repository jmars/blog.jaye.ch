# Undreaming Wetiko

### One book, read at its own scale — the collapse named as a contagion, a cosmology with "no substantial existence from its own side" that can still kill, and a remedy that is the blog's own boundary

*Third of the `readings`, and the collective one. [The holy daimon](/the-holy-daimon/) and
[the holy heretics](/the-holy-heretics/) read Frater Acher against the corpus's mechanism — one
practitioner, then one institution. This reads Paul Levy's *Undreaming Wetiko*, the third volume of
his trilogy on wetiko, and the scale changes in one specific way: the collapse this blog has
described inside a single system arrives here as something that **travels between persons** — it
"germinates in and replicates itself through the unconscious of the children". [The mechanism
post](/meditation-harm/) set the collapse out; [the environment](/the-environment/) set out what a
surrounding culture supplies a predator; [the costume](/the-costume/) stripped the levers to
positions; [the follower](/the-follower/) dissolved the type; [the operative
master](/the-operative-master/) took it down to one operator inside one frame. The book does two
things none of them could: it names the mechanism as **contagious**, and it states the corpus's own
boundary — *describe the structure, diagnose no one* — as its **method**, in its own voice, before
the blog could.*

*Status.* This post reads **one** source. The quotations are verbatim from the digital text, checked
character for character against the file and cited in the notes by part and chapter and by the
digital text's own page markers; the reading of them is an **inference**, offered as one. **The
book's cosmology is not endorsed** — nothing here claims that wetiko is a real entity, and nothing
claims it is only a metaphor; §3 is about why holding that open is the point. No living author is
diagnosed; the appropriation question in §6 is described as a structure and **not adjudicated** in
either direction; and nothing here validates, disqualifies or restricts anyone's practice. As
everywhere: describe structures, diagnose no one.

## 1. The book, and the trilogy it closes

*Undreaming Wetiko* is by Paul Levy, published by Inner Traditions in 2023 — print ISBN
978-1-64411-566-4, ebook 978-1-64411-567-1.[^uw] It is the **third** book in his wetiko trilogy, and
the afterword says so: the publisher's Sacred Planet Books imprint began "with his second volume on
the topic, *Wetiko: Healing the Mind-Virus That Plagues Our World*, and now this third folio,
*Undreaming Wetiko*".[^aw]

The term is not his, and the book is exact about where it came from — the introduction credits the
source by name, book and year:

> "having just learned about it from writer, scholar, and political activist Jack D. Forbes, in his
> 1979 book *Columbus and Other Cannibals: The Wetiko Disease of Exploitation, Imperialism, and
> Terrorism*."[^intro]

Before he had the word he had coined his own — "having coined the term malignant egophrenia, or ME
disease"[^intro] — and the back matter keeps both: "ME disease: malignant egophrenia. ME disease
was my original name for what I later discovered the Native Americans called wetiko."[^gloss]

Its structure is four parts: **Spells, Curses, and Shamans**; **Angels, Demons, and Rebirth**; **The
Quantum Synchronistic Field**; **The Self as Revelation**. The fourth is where this reading turns,
because its two adjacent chapter titles are the book's argument in miniature — **"The Disease of Not
Seeing"** and **"The Art of Seeing"**.[^uw] And before all of it sits a dedication to a person:
"This book is dedicated to the great doctor of the soul C. G. Jung."[^ded]

That dedication page is not incidental. It places the lineage: "Jung, though not having the
indigenous name, was tracking the spore-prints of wetiko as it weaved itself throughout the warp and
woof of the human psyche and the world at large."[^ded] So the book's account of where it comes from
is three-stranded — an Algonquian term through Forbes, a name of Levy's own, and a psychiatrist
tracking the thing under another description, made explicit in the chapter where the naming matters
most: "Jung was tracking this mind-virus, not calling it wetiko, but mainly referring to it as the
'totalitarian psychosis.'"[^ch12]

## 2. The mechanism, on a field of persons

[The mechanism post](/meditation-harm/) describes one system: sustained inward attention, the
generator of self-content collapsing, a state whose reading cannot be reported from inside it, and an
appraisal supplied from whatever stands on the far side. The finding that makes it hard is that the
apparatus which would notice the failure **is the apparatus that survives it** — so the person saying
*this is freedom* is not lying, and is accurate from the only vantage they have left.

*Undreaming Wetiko* describes a collapse with the same shape and a **different substrate**. Its
unit is not one nervous system. Its unit is a field of persons, and the thing it names moves through
that field without anyone choosing it:

> "The repressed and unlived lives of the parents act like a contagious and malignant psychic virus
> that infects the surrounding field."[^ch1]

> "This virulent psychic pathogen germinates in and replicates itself through the unconscious of the
> children, which is the medium it uses to reproduce itself over time, through the generations."[^ch1]

That is the same mechanism at the scale this series has not had a name for. The book gives it three
registers, and each is worth holding separately.

**Down a generation.** The chapter is titled "Human Family Curse", and its claim is that what is
transmitted is not a doctrine but an **unconscious**, because the child's medium is the parent's
unspoken material: whatever the parents repressed is "nonetheless alive, covertly working in the
surrounding environment".[^ch1] That is the corpus's *cannot be reported from inside* located in a
different place: the person who carries the content did not put it there and cannot read it.

**Sideways, between two people.** The book's set-piece is two friends, Jim and Sally, both on a
spiritual path, in which each one's unconscious reading of the other confirms itself:

> "Jim is now dreaming up Sally in such a way as to play out with her an inner process of which he is
> unconscious, while Sally's unconscious is doing the same with Jim. They are co-dreaming."[^ch2]

Note the structure: each party is **sincere**, each is certain the other is the one who is not
seeing, and "because this process is playing out through Jim's blind spot, which has become activated
by seeing Sally's blind spot, he is not open to having his unconscious process reflected back to
him…".[^ch2] The far side of a state that cannot report itself is here a **person**, not a group —
which is [the operative master](/the-operative-master/)'s one-to-one frame, arrived at from the
other end.

**Across a population.** The book's largest register is the one the word itself came with: "The
disease of wetiko is infectious; its presence can potentially activate the latent plague in
ordinarily decent people."[^ch3] And in the chapter on not seeing, a physician's description of what
is happening worldwide is adopted as the term of art: "McCullough is precisely describing the wetiko
mind-virus, a contagious mental psychosis."[^ch12]

**The reading, and it is this post's.** This is a **second substrate** for the corpus's mechanism —
not a new mechanism, and not a stronger claim about any individual. The consequence is narrow and
real: the corpus's account of how a state comes to be misread has never needed a person to be
malicious, and at this scale it does not even need the far side to be one person. The appraisal can
arrive from a field.

**The counterweight, in the body, because it is the whole risk here.** Called "contagious", the
mechanism becomes a way of describing *other people as vectors*, and the book knows it: §5 is the
chapter in which it forbids exactly that. And the transmission described above is a claim inside the
book's model; nothing here has measured it. What can be reported exactly is that the unit of
transmission is the unconscious, that the carriers are described as unaware, and that the author's
own word for it is pathological.

## 3. Entity or structure — the centre of the reading

Here is the sentence this reading turns on, and it turns on both halves at once:

> "Wetiko has no intrinsic, independent existence (separate from the mind, that is), which is to say
> that it has no substantial existence from its own side; and yet it can wreak unimaginable havoc
> and even kill us."[^intro]

Set against the rest of the book, that is not one claim. It is two, held together deliberately, and
the book does not resolve them. On the one side, wetiko is described as an agent with intentions and
a will — "a master impersonator—we can conceive of it as aping the Divine", and the *Apocryphon of
John*'s name for it, "the counterfeiting spirit".[^intro] It has a policy: "It's as if wetiko has its
own propaganda department dedicated to keeping itself hidden. More than anything, wetiko hates to be
outed, as it only has power when it works in the shadows of our minds."[^intro] It is personified:
"Satan, the personification of wetiko".[^ch3] And it has a phenomenology, stated in the chapter on
shamans as an encounter: "Our subjective experience of the demon of sickness is an encounter with a
**seemingly autonomous**, life-destroying entity whose intent is to take us down."[^ch4]

On the other side, the book says plainly that it has no existence of its own, and it locates the
whole thing in a process rather than a being: "The genesis of wetiko is to be found deep within our
minds. It is a dreaming phenomenon, which is to say that it is something that in my language we are
dreaming up, both collectively, in the world, and in our individual minds."[^intro] The Introduction
puts it in a phrase: wetiko is "a misidentification of who we think we are".[^intro]

**This is the corpus's frame question, arriving in the book's own vocabulary.** [The
environment](/the-environment/)'s first lever is **a cosmology of hidden agency** — a place in a
system where a claim is unobservable and authoritative at once, and the predator is the one who can
read it. Is wetiko that lever? The corpus's tool for the question is not to answer it. A frame is
described by **what it does**, and a reading of this series never adopts the cosmology it describes.
So both poles are refused here, for a reason each:

- **Not "it is a real demon."** That would be adopting a cosmology, which is the one prohibition this
  series holds absolutely — and nothing in this post adjudicates what wetiko is.
- **Not "it is only a metaphor."** That is a metaphysical verdict too, and it is one the book refuses
  before the reader does: the afterword's author holds the same tension explicitly, and in the same breath.
  "You could say it's not real, it's a metaphor, an exaggeration, a fantastic legend … I would say
  yes, of course, but also that it's real on an energetic level."[^aw] He also calls it "a sort of
  'true' sorcery"[^aw] and prints M. Scott Peck's conclusion from *People of the Lie* — "This was a
  wake-up call for Peck: **the devil is real**."[^aw]

**What is left, once both poles are refused, is a structural reading, and it is this post's.** A
thing that "has no substantial existence from its own side" and can still kill is not a contradiction
in a book about a virus. It is a **position with no occupant** — and that is exactly the shape of
every lever this series has described. [The costume](/the-costume/) found the operative element of
hidden agency to be "the **unfalsifiable slot** — the place in a system where a claim is at once
unobservable and authoritative", and found that positions are "indifferent to what is poured into
them". *Undreaming Wetiko* runs the same structure at the scale of a self. The slot is in a mind; the
thing poured into it is an identity; and because the slot does not supply its own content, the
content has to come from somewhere.

The book states that consequence in the passage that belongs beside [the mechanism
post](/meditation-harm/), because it is the *cannot report itself* written as an interrogation:

> "Is it us or the counterfeiting spirit, wetiko, impersonating us who is deciding?"[^ch10]

**Counterweight, and it is not optional here.** This reading is a **reduction** — it takes a book
that speaks of Satan and angels and reads it as a book about unfalsifiable positions — and the book
would not accept the reduction. That matters, because the two halves are load-bearing for different
parts of it: the "no substantial existence" half underwrites the remedy (if it has no existence of
its own, seeing it dissolves it), and the "seemingly autonomous entity" half underwrites the
demonology — Jung's demon of sickness, the *Apocryphon*, Peck, and a chapter on the battle for one's
angel. A post that quoted only the first half would be quoting the book it has improved into
agreement with itself. Both halves are here, and §8 draws the correction from the first half only.

## 4. The cure is seeing

The remedy is not hidden in this book, and it is not subtle. It is stated as a diagnosis and as a
chapter title, and it is repeated in every register the book has:

> "Wetiko, after all is said and done, is an all-around form of psychic blindness in which neither
> its perpetrators nor its victims are spared. **This is why the cure for wetiko is to see it.**"[^ch3]

> "Being a form of psychic blindness, wetiko, as I can't mention often enough, **only has power over
> us to the extent it is not seen**."[^ch2]

> "Wetiko is a form of psychic blindness. At a certain point in time we chose to look away—to blind
> ourselves—so as to avoid being in relationship to a part of ourselves. **Our turning away is
> wetiko.**"[^ch13]

And the epilogue's version, which is the sharpest, because it is a description of a state rather than
an instruction: "wetiko is a form of psychospiritual blindness. In a very real sense, humanity has
fallen blind, and we don't even realize that we are blind."[^epi] What is offered is not insight into
a doctrine but a **modality**: "we suffer from a form of hysterical blindness that we ourselves are
inducing and maintaining, not knowing that our inner eyes can see."[^epi]

Two more versions state it as a **medicine**. The introduction's is the diagnostic half: "Because
wetiko disease basically means to have fallen into a state of mistaken identity, the best medicine
for wetiko is to know who we are."[^intro] The synopsis that opens Part 4 gives the prescriptive
half: "the best medicine for the wetiko mind-virus is to connect with the presence and light of our
true nature, by stepping into who we truly are."[^part4] Both are the same claim, and it is not an
operation performed on the virus but a **relation to what is not the virus**.

**This is the corpus's own conclusion, and the book reaches it independently.** Two other posts
arrived at the same place from different directions. [The cosmos that
refuses](/the-cosmos-that-refuses/) ended on the finding that **no property of a cosmology is self-enforcing** —
"the safeguard was never in the cosmology", and an unfalsifiable refusal is a seat waiting to happen.
[The operative master](/the-operative-master/) ended with the rule this blog now writes by:
"**concealment cannot warn anyone**" — the reader who most needs the shape cannot be reached by
withholding it, only made **legible**. *Undreaming Wetiko* states the same remedy as the object's own
property and then states the mechanism's own reason for it, in the introduction: wetiko "avoids the
light of awareness like the plague".[^intro] A mechanism that is dissolved by being seen is a
mechanism that is *described* by the corpus's decision to publish rather than withhold.

There is one more convergence, and it is exact. The mechanism post's structural fact is that the
capacity which reports survives the collapse; the person can still say something, but cannot report
the state. The book's remedy rests on precisely that survivor:

> "The part of us that sees our blindness is the part of us that is beginning to see."[^ch13]

Read together, the two are saying the same thing about the same organ: there is no vantage outside a
collapse, and there is a part that notices — and the whole remedy is addressed to it.

**The counterweight, and it is the gap this reading owes.** Seeing is a **first-person act**. Levy's
remedy is a person looking at their own blindness, and the corpus's hardest finding is that this is
the one thing the apparatus cannot do: the instrument that would audit is the instrument under audit,
and [the container](/the-container/) is the corpus's clearest statement of why. The book's answer is
that the *unwounded* part sees — which is a real answer, and it is still the same person. Nothing in
this book supplies the second reader that [the follower](/the-follower/) and [the holy
heretics](/the-holy-heretics/) both ended up requiring, and §7 says so in the text's own terms.

## 5. The detector trap — the boundary the book states as its method

Now the strongest thing in this reading, and it is a rule the book writes for itself, in the chapter
called "The Art of Seeing", immediately before it explains how to teach anyone anything:

> "When we see people who have fallen under the spell of wetiko, it's very tempting to think of them
> in any number of ways—they're ignorant, crazy, or maybe somewhat evil themselves. But this would be
> a mistake."[^ch13]

> "To mischaracterize them is to not see them accurately (remember, wetiko is a form of blindness).
> This furthers the separation, polarization, and misunderstandings, and hence **not only feeds
> wetiko, but is wetiko in action**."[^ch13]

And the second, which is the diagnostic half turned on the diagnostician:

> "To judge someone this way is a reflection that we are in a **solidified** (as compared to fluid)
> state ourselves, which only furthers the dominion of wetiko in the collective field."[^ch13]

Sit with the shape of that. The corpus's standing boundary — restated at every scale — is that
**levers without the mechanism are not predation**: [the costume](/the-costume/) drew it to refuse
becoming a cult-detector, and [the label post](/the-label/) exists because the sorting operation
tells you about the sorter. *Undreaming Wetiko* says the same thing, one turn harder. It does not
say the label is unkind, or inattentive, or a heuristic that can misfire. It says the labelling move
**is the disease**.

**The corpus's discipline and the book's method are the same sentence**, and it is unusual for a
source in this series to hand the blog its own rule back. It also sharpens what [the operative
master](/the-operative-master/) left as a procedural choice. There, withholding the gauging was a
decision made *for* the reader, on the ground that a page teaching the fit helps an operator and not
a target. Here the reason is structural rather than editorial: the mischaracterisation has an effect
in the field, and the effect is the thing being described. The boundary is not a courtesy this blog
extends; on the book's account it is part of the countermeasure.

**And here is the counterweight, in the body, because the rule is harder than the text that states
it.** A book that forbids the label does not thereby stop naming enemies, and this one does not. The
same chapter opens by saying that "the dark forces of the universe are coming out of hiding and are
visible for all who have eyes to see",[^ch12] and goes on to name totalitarianism and its mutant
form; and it gives the trap its own acronym, "not see disease (acronym NSD), which it should be
pointed out is pronounced the same as 'Nazi disease.'"[^ch12] So the rule against diagnosing persons
is held inside a text that is at war with something and says so. That is not a contradiction to be
tidied. It is the discipline stated as a **discipline** — a line the author has to keep while writing
about a thing he believes is destroying the world — and the corpus's own rule requires exactly that
reading of it: the text describes structures, and where it names a structure it may, and where it
would sort a person it may not.

**And so this post does not use the book as a detector**, does not adjudicate whether wetiko is real,
and does not treat any reader, group or author as a carrier. The next section is the boundary this
reading has to hold for a different reason.

## 6. The boundary risk — an outside frame that arrived with its warrant already issued

Levy is a non-indigenous author writing the third of three books about a term from an Algonquian
language group, and the book does not leave that unaddressed. It closes with an afterword by Richard
Grossinger — a publisher and anthropologist — whose title is the whole problem in ten words:
**"Paul Levy's Conversion of First Nations Cannibalism to Global Sociopathy"**.[^aw] Grossinger
states the objection and does not bury it:

> "I was dubious about a white dude elucidating a Native American concept."[^aw]

Then he answers it, and he issues his warrant openly:

> "Yes, he 'appropriated.' But then, using the wisdom traditions of Buddhism, analytical psychology,
> anthroposophy, and quantum physics, he translated and transmitted the skrying from its native
> woodlands and plains."[^aw]

> "Wetiko's subsequent planetarization made a clinical outlier like Paul a new sort of global
> psycholinguist and psychotherapist, and **an exception to 'paleface' appropriation**."[^aw]

And he names what he thinks he found: "Over time, and at a deeper level as a result of writing this
afterword, I have come to understand the true power of Paul's 'appropriation.'"[^aw]

**This is a structural position, and the corpus already has its shape.** [The
environment](/the-environment/)'s fourth lever is **a pre-certified frame**: "the warrant is issued
by the *host*, not the group, and scrutiny reads as bigotry", and its named instance is "the
diaspora's exotic warrant". [The certified frame](/the-certified-frame/) took that lever up on its
own, and its finding is that the warrant arrives before the teacher does — and that the check on it
is the very thing the warrant immunises. What is in this book is that structure with a variation
worth naming precisely: **the warrant is issued by a sympathetic outsider, in the book, on the
record.** The frame travels (an indigenous term becomes a Western cosmology), and it does not arrive
uncertified; it arrives with a certificate, written by a party who is not the term's community,
printed inside the object it certifies.

**Two things the corpus's rule requires here, and both hold.**

**No diagnosis of Levy or Grossinger.** What is quoted above is what they wrote and what the structure
is. Nothing in this post is a finding about either man's intentions, competence, sincerity or
standing, and nothing here is a claim about what Grossinger privately thinks. [The
label](/the-label/)'s rule is not suspended because the subject is appropriation.

**No adjudication.** Whether the borrowing is legitimate; whether Grossinger's defence succeeds;
whether "an exception" is a category the grantor can grant; what Forbes or Algonquian speakers would
say — none of it is decided here in either direction. A reading that resolved it would be issuing a
verdict about a practice from outside, on evidence it does not have, about people it has not
consulted.

**The counterweight, and it must not be flattened.** A pre-issued warrant is usually a closed thing;
this one is not entirely closed. The doubt is printed — "I was dubious about a white dude elucidating
a Native American concept" — which is the opposite of what a frame that immunises itself against
scrutiny does. The philology is printed too, including the argument against the author's own
preference: Grossinger gives "wetiko ('wet-tee-ko' …)" as the author's, against "('wet-tick-ko', as
Western Algonquian linguists propose)", and then says that "neither version is precisely Algonquian,
Cree, or Powhatan".[^aw] And the book prints an indigenous author's own description of the entity,
in his words, not in paraphrase: "The Wendigo was gaunt to the point of emaciation, its desiccated
skin pulled tightly over its bones."[^aw]

So the structure is **not** a hidden warrant. It is a warrant issued openly, by a named outsider,
inside a text that also prints the objection and the linguistic counter-evidence — a different
position from [the certified frame](/the-certified-frame/)'s closed one, and a reading that called
the two the same would be flattening evidence to fit a pattern. What they share is the property this
series cares about: **the check that would have to come from the term's own community is, in the
structure of the text, made in advance by someone else**, and the reader is handed a resolved
question. §8 draws no correction from this, and it is recorded because a reading that reported the
contagion and the remedy and omitted the warrant would be describing a cleaner book than the one on
the shelf.

## 7. Two counterweights and one gap, in the body

The series requires an explicit counterweight or gap, in the body, and this book supplies three
things the argument has to carry rather than file in the notes.

**First, the demonology is real in the text, and it is not a costume.** The corpus's habit is to
treat a cosmology as a costume over levers; this is the case where the costume is load-bearing: Satan
as "the personification of wetiko",[^ch3] "the demon of sickness" from Jung,[^ch4] the "autonomous
complex" that "can go rogue and become an antilife force, an agent of death",[^ch4] angels in Part
II, the risk of "falling prey to the seductive, entrancing, demonic spell of wetiko",[^ch5] and an
afterword's author prints that "the devil is real".[^aw] A reading that reported only the "no
substantial existence" passage would be quoting a tidier book. The honest report is that *Undreaming
Wetiko* is a modern demonology built by a Jungian Buddhist around an indigenous term, and that the
blog, which describes frames without adopting them, has to report that fact rather than resolve it
into the structure it prefers.

**Second, the remedy's instrument is the same instrument the corpus says cannot audit itself.** §4
states it and the book's own words make it exact — the part that sees the blindness "is the part of
us that is beginning to see",[^ch13] which is a real claim about a real witness and is still the
same person. The corpus's requirement is a reading held by someone else, and nothing between these
covers supplies one. This is the reading's gap, and it is the one thing the book's own chapter on
seeing does not close.

**Third, and it is a gap reported the way the series reports absences: as a search result.** The word
"safeguard" occurs **once** in this text, and it is not a safeguard of the reader: "In order to
safeguard what is most sacred—life itself—we need to know what threatens it."[^ch3] The clinical
referral is absent: a case-insensitive search of the digital text returns **0** hits for "seek help",
"professional help", "support group", "hotline", "crisis line", "self-harm", "suicid", "emergency",
"antipsychotic" and "spiritual emergency", and the four occurrences of "clinical" and the single
occurrence of "clinician" are all in the afterword.[^abs]

That absence is **not silence about medicine**, and the difference matters. Where the previous two
readings found nothing said about clinicians, this book **names psychiatry as an adversary** — the
place where the blindness took physical form. The epilogue reports the author's own entry into it in
the first person: "the **hallowed—and maddening—halls of psychiatry**",[^epi] and a diagnosis
refused, "the diagnosis itself being what was pathological".[^epi] The back matter gives the same
history in the third person: "he was hospitalized a number of times and was told he was having a
severe psychotic break from reality and was (mis)diagnosed as having a chemical imbalance … he was
taking part in a spiritual awakening and shamanic initiation, which at times mimicked psychosis but
in actuality was a spiritual experience of a far different order".[^author]

Read exactly, that is a text-level structure and not a finding about any person's mind. The corpus's
referral question is not answered here; it is raised from the other side, and two readings have now
reported the same absence. This third supplies a reason the absence might be deliberate: in this
cosmology, an institution that reads a revelation as an illness is an instance of the blindness the
book is about. That is reported as the text reports it, and it is not a finding about the psychiatric
system or about the author.

## 8. What the rest of the blog owes

Three corrections.

**First, and it is the one this post is for: the mechanism's substrate is not only one system.**
[The environment](/the-environment/)'s five levers are what an environment supplies **to a predator**
— the price of predation, lowered by a culture that has already built the supports. *Undreaming
Wetiko* supplies the complementary claim, and the corpus did not have it: the field itself carries the
collapse, and it does so with **no predator in the loop at all**. The parents are not predators; the
book says their repression is "covertly working in the surrounding environment" and that the child's
unconscious is "the medium it uses to reproduce itself over time, through the generations".[^ch1] Jim
and Sally are two sincere friends, and they co-dream each other anyway.[^ch2] The correction is
therefore to the sweep of the account rather than to any of its parts:

> **The mechanism has a second substrate — a field of persons — and on that substrate it needs no
> holder, no group and no frame-builder. The environment post described what lowers the price of
> predation. Levy describes the same collapse being carried by people who are not preying on anyone.**

That is a genuine addition: the mechanism's account needed an appraisal from the far side, and this
gives it a far side that can be **a field** rather than a person standing over someone. It weakens no
case study in the series, and it removes an assumption none of them stated — that where there is a
collapse, someone has to be steering it.

**Second, [the follower](/the-follower/)'s dissolve covers the enemy as well as the recruit.** That
post took the follower **type** apart and left a position that a condition activates, on the strength
of a literature that could not locate the thing in the person. *Undreaming Wetiko* performs the same
move on the other half of the pair, and it is the sharpest passage in the book for this series:

> "It's always some form of 'the other.' Some people even have the temerity to suggest the radical
> idea that these dark forces are ultimately to be found within our own minds, a perspective that
> gets closer to the mark than any other perspective."[^ch12]

The named-list is the method: "the Republicans, the Democrats, the atheists, the fundamentalists, the
terrorists, the Left, the Right, the liberals, the conservatives, the pro-vaxxers, the
antivaxxers, the Illuminati, the globalists, and on and on."[^ch12] Every one of those is an
outside-reading adopted to avoid an inner one — the same figure as §3's empty slot, filled from
outside so it does not have to be looked at from within. The corpus has dissolved the follower; it
can now say that **the enemy dissolves the same way**, and with the same consequence: what is left is
a position and a misidentification, not a kind of person. That is a correction the blog owes, and it
is one the corpus's own rule already implied.

**Third, [the operative master](/the-operative-master/)'s decision to publish the shape gains the

## 9. What a reading is for

A reading holds one source **whole** — its lineage, its structure, its demonology, its remedy, its
omissions, its afterword — and asks what a blog that has quoted sources for years now has to change.

What this one says is a convergence and a gap, and the pair is the honest result.

**The convergence is the strongest in the series so far.** A source the blog has never quoted
arrives independently at the corpus's two hardest findings: that a state can be unreadable from
inside and misread from the far side, and that **seeing is the remedy** — not a doctrine, not a
frame, not a cosmology, but legibility. It reaches the second one from the object's side, which is
where no other source has reached it, and it arrives at the blog's own boundary while doing so:
mischaracterising a person "not only feeds wetiko, but is wetiko in action". A book about a virus
that cannot be located says, in its central chapter, that the locating move is the virus.

**The gap is unchanged and is not closed.** The remedy is a first-person act, and the corpus's finding
is that the instrument which would audit is the instrument under audit. This book supplies the
witness — the part that sees the blindness — and not the **second reader**, which is what
[the cosmos that refuses](/the-cosmos-that-refuses/) said the refusal still needed, what [the holy
heretics](/the-holy-heretics/) found missing from a book with a criterion, and what [the operative
master](/the-operative-master/) named as the check an operator fails by construction. And the
outside frame arrives with its warrant already issued by a sympathetic party, printed inside the
book — a structure this reading records without resolving, because resolving it is a verdict about
people, and issuing verdicts is the move this book has just told us *is* the disease.

Which leaves the reading's own bottom line, and it is a sentence this source hands back almost
unrepaired, in a chapter about the temptation to diagnose: *to describe a structure is to work on
oneself; to sort a person with it is to become a vector.* The corpus has been saying that at every
