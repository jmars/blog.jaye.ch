# Quareia

### Fourteenth of the `readings`, one curriculum at its own scale — the course the blog has been quoting in fragments since its first week, read whole, and found to state the blog's own claim about curricula in its own words, to carry a stronger clinical procedure than the blog credited it with, and to leave the check that decides everything to the practitioner

*Fourteenth of the `readings`, and the first whose unit is a **training** rather than a book. Every reading
before this one held a single volume up to the series' test. This one holds up fourteen — five apprentice
books, five initiate books, four adept books — and the claim being tested is the blog's most confident one
about the West. [The western column](/the-western-column/) put it in a line: **a curriculum is a
safeguard.** It was an inference from extracts. [The holy daimon](/the-holy-daimon/) had already narrowed it
(a book with no gate has nowhere to put a referral); [Egregores](/egregores/) had already leaned on it; the
[frame](/the-frame-is-a-variable/) series supplied the test underneath it and [the
differential](/the-differential/) the boundary it is measured against. Here the claim meets the curriculum's
own account of what it does — and the curriculum says the same thing, in a sentence the blog never quoted.*

*Status.* This post reads **one** source: the Quareia curriculum of Josephine McCarthy, in the
whitespace-collapsed course scans listed in [^course] and the per-book scans beside them. Quotations are
verbatim, checked character for character and cited in the notes **by grade, book, module, lesson and the
scan's own page marker**; the reading of them is an **inference**, offered as one. **The curriculum's
cosmology is not endorsed** — nothing here claims its inner realms, deities, contacts or adept magicians
exist, and nothing claims they do not. No living author is diagnosed, no practitioner's experience is
validated or disqualified, and its terms — *adept*, *initiate*, *contact* — are reported in its sense, not
adopted.*

---

## 1. What the curriculum is, and why the blog's claim has to be tested against it

*Quareia* is a graded magical training by Josephine McCarthy, published free online by Quareia Publishing UK
in fourteen volumes: Books One to Five carry the **Apprentice** section, Six to Ten the **Initiate**, Eleven
to Fourteen the **Adept**.[^course] Each section is ten modules of eight lessons; the adept books carry the
adept modules two to a volume.[^structure] Every lesson opens with a welcome page stating the course's
purpose in a sentence repeated, unchanged, eighty-one times in the apprentice scan alone:

> "The Quareia takes a magical apprentice from the beginning of magic to the level of adeptship and beyond.
> The course has no superfluous text; there is no dressing, no padding — everything is in its place and
> everything within the course has a good reason to be there."[^wel]

A route from the beginning to adeptship and past it, with nothing in it that is not load-bearing — and the
second sentence of the same page is the one the blog has leaned on since its first week:

> "So remember - in order for this course to work, it is wise to work with the lessons in sequence. If you
> don't, it won't work."[^wel]

Read in the place the file puts it, that is a **causal** claim about efficacy, not a protective claim about
the student: the work depends on the order, and out of order it "won't work". Safety is not mentioned yet. It
arrives, and it arrives in the curriculum's own words — which is the finding this reading is built on.

Three borrowings make the reading necessary. [The western column](/the-western-column/) took the pace
("**solid, safe and sane**… in **slow, careful steps**"), the deferral of other-lives work ("the **ego
inflation and fantasy realm** that a magician can so easily get stuck in") and the clinical referral
("**if you are still hearing them, go see a doctor**").[^wc] [Egregores](/egregores/) used the third as the
contrast that indicts another book, and [the holy daimon](/the-holy-daimon/) used it again as the gap that
book's material invites. All three are accurate — worth saying, because the series' usual finding is
otherwise — and all three came from extracts.

**And the debt.** The test applied here is not the blog's invention. *Does the frame supply a check that does
not benefit from the answer* comes from the frames series; the boundary the referral is measured against
comes from [the differential](/the-differential/). This post's own contribution is the comparison only.

## 2. The structure, and the text's own account of what the structure is for

Start with what the order is, because the blog's word for it — a gate at each rung — underdescribes it. **The
order is a requirement to complete before advancing, stated as one** — in the welcome page, and again where
the stakes are highest. In the apprentice section's practical destruction work, contacted ritual with inner
contacts specified in the text, the lesson opens with a fence:

> "Note: if you are not a Quareia student and have not done all of the lessons of the apprentice section so
> far, please do not do this work, **for your own safety**."[^app107]

That is the blog's claim in the curriculum's own voice, with the words attached: not *it will not work*, but
*do not do this*. It is the only place in the apprentice section where the sequence requirement gets a
safety rationale rather than an efficacy one.

**The order is also metered.** The advisory opening each apprentice book is four numbered rules; two are
about rate:

> "1. Pace yourself. Some modules can be done together, some cannot… **There is no clock ticking, and it is
> not a race.**"[^adv]

The fourth makes the same demand of the student: "Do not skim through the lessons. You simply will not learn
and you will not properly develop. Slow down, take your time."[^adv] That is a metered pace *without a
meter*: a rate prescription whose stated purpose is comprehension, and whose only enforcement is the
student's work.

**And the pace is content-gated, in a way the blog can check concretely.** The flame meditation that later
becomes "the Void" is not the first exercise of the first lesson. It is the third, held behind a capacity
threshold stated in the same breath:

> "Once you are accustomed to visualising in meditation and are able to sit for half an hour or more, then
> it is time to learn the meditation of the inner flame."[^app11]

> "This image will be slowly connected, through your training, to a state of mind and a state of power
> called the Void… it is the nothing from which all things flow."[^app11]

So the blog's line — *Quareia will not give you the Void's deeper uses until you have done the earlier work* —
is right in substance and loose in detail: what the file shows is a **capacity threshold** gating a
**technique**, in the first week of the first module. The gate is not a person saying no. It is a condition
printed on the page.

**The order's stated purpose includes protecting the work — a different thing from protecting the student.**
The apprentice mid-term summary explains the no-skimming rule from the inside:

> "If someone browses through the lessons, cherry-picks what work they want to do and ignores the rest, they
> will miss these subtle prompts and keys. This in turn **protects the work, and protects the casual
> browser**."[^app68]

The consequence is stated as a rule of design rather than of supervision: "the 'pickers and dippers' will be
**filtered out by their own unwillingness** to take the time to read, digest, and work."[^app68] The design
protects **the work** and **the browser**; it names no supervisor, examiner or body. The mechanism is the
text's refusal to be skimmable, and the outcome it predicts is self-selection: the filter is the student's
own unwillingness. Nobody stands at the door. The door is heavy.

**The ego-inflation deferral the blog quoted is where the blog said it was.** Verified, apprentice Module 8,
Lesson 6, on the northern root temples:

> "But when it comes to accessing your other lives, it is best to wait until you are in the adept phase of
> your training. This is not because such work is dangerous, but because of the ego inflation and fantasy
> realm that a magician can so easily get stuck in. Many try to relive these other lives, preferring them to
> their present life: that is just immature escapism."[^app86]

The reason is psychological, not metaphysical: not that the operation is unsafe but that the practitioner
gets stuck in the fantasy of it — and *preferring the other life to this one* is not the kind of thing any
mentor in the record adjudicates.

**And the curriculum states its own protective mechanism, in one sentence, at the end of the grade.** After
telling the reader that initiation in a lodge cannot be self-administered, the apprentice section's last
lesson gives its own account of what Quareia's version is protected by:

> "And this is how Quareia protects itself. If someone who has not done the work reads this lesson and tries
> to do it, they will **fail miserably**, as the keys that unlock this gate were embedded in the magician
> throughout their apprentice training… This is why there is no need for secrecy: **the Mysteries protect
> themselves.** They blossom within the worker, and evade the curious."[^app109]

**That is the centre of this reading.** The blog said *a curriculum is a safeguard*. The curriculum says
*this is how the curriculum protects itself*, and names the mechanism: the keys are embedded in the worker by
the doing, so the gate cannot be opened by someone who has not done it — not because a person refuses them,
not because a rank is withheld, but because the thing is inert in the wrong hands. §3 asks what instruments it
supplies for the cases where that is not enough. §4 asks what the claim cannot reach.

## 3. The checks: what is actually in the file

The file is more specific than the blog credited on one axis and less specific on the others. Take the
clinical referral first — and here the passage the blog quotes is a **differential, not an aside**. It sits
in apprentice Module 1, Lesson 5, "Inner Senses", where, before anything positive is said about inner
hearing, the text rules out what looks like it: tinnitus under the name musical hallucination, with its
triggers ("brain tumours, epilepsy, migraines") and its marker (remembered childhood music), and then:

> "The other biological event that can be mistaken for inner hearing is symptoms of paranoid schizophrenia.
> In such cases there is often a whole array of mental health symptoms that help to rule out inner
> hearing."[^app15]

Only then comes the discipline, stated as a **sequence**:

> "As with all inner senses, you can immediately see the dangers of confusing inner senses with mental
> obsessions and illnesses, and as with all things magical, you must approach such things intelligently,
> carefully and with common sense. **If you hear voices telling you to kill people, you have a problem.
> Clean yourself up magically, and if you are still hearing them, go see a doctor.**"[^app15]

The blog's reading of that — *try the tradition's remedy, and if it fails, leave the tradition* — is correct
at the level of the sentence. What the lesson adds is what the blog never had: a **rule-out procedure**
first, so that the referral is not a concession bolted onto a magical claim but the second half of a
differential.

**And it is not one sentence. It is a procedure, at four grades of the curriculum, with an order and a scope
rule.** Set out as the file has them.

*Grade one — apprentice, inner senses (above):* try the remedy first; if the symptom persists, a doctor.

*Grade two — apprentice, on parasites.* Inner infestation and mental illness "are often very similar, to the
point that it can be difficult to tell the difference — and there is a difference", and then the triage
rule:

> "**Someone who is mentally ill and not infested needs to be treated medically. Someone who is infested
> needs magical treatment. Sometimes, both treatments are needed concurrently.** But as apprentices, you are
> not yet adequately equipped to deal with such a situation."[^app64]

*Grade three — initiate, exorcism, where the curriculum's own practitioners meet the case that looks like
possession.* This is the strongest instrument in the record: the misidentification is named as a
presentation that "can even fool a doctor if they have little or no knowledge of the occult or religion",
and then the finding:

> "But they are not; they are dealing with **a very ill person**. And it is really important to be able to
> make that distinction, because such a person is in need of **immediate and highly specialised medical
> care**."[^ini21]

And the magician's role in that case is refused outright:

> "The treatment of such illnesses is still in the early phase of understanding in medical terms, but **the
> role of the exorcist has no place in that treatment, and can often make the situation worse**."[^ini21]

*Grade four — initiate, basic exorcism, the scope rule.* Here the curriculum forbids the practitioner to
make the assessment at all, and orders it outside:

> "Unless you have worked in mental health or have a lot of experience in mental health, **do not try to
> assess the person's mental health**. That is the work of an adept and a psychologist or psychiatrist. The
> presentation of mental illness and/or possession can be the same to an untrained eye, and is something
> that must be approached properly: **first is a mental health assessment by a psychiatrist.**… Should you
> come up against someone showing signs of full possession, you must walk away from that situation and
> advise them to get a mental health assessment before doing anything else: making a mistake can do the
> victim a great deal of long-term damage."[^ini25]

The same module adds an **ordering rule** and a prior-exhaustion rule:

> "Of course all medical avenues must have also been explored."[^ini26]

> "Though mental issues are to be left to a trained psychotherapist… But remember: **your job is inner
> repair, not psychological repair.** Otherwise you can end up doing more harm than good."[^ini82]

Read the four grades together: the curriculum distinguishes a category of problem the practice cannot
address, assigns the assessment to a named profession, **forbids the practitioner to make it**, orders the
professions medical-first, and instructs withdrawal and referral in the hardest case — a **triage protocol**,
and the instrument [the differential](/the-differential/) described from the clinic's side.

**The second axis is the "stop if" rule, and there are real ones** — the clearest a health-status exclusion
in the initiate exorcism module:

> "**Note 1: Do not do this work if you are pregnant, have small children at home, or are sick — even if it
> is only a minor illness.**"[^ini26]

A second excludes a household rather than a state ("do not do this exercise if you have children in the
house");[^ini18] a third is the sequence fence quoted in §2;[^app107] a fourth closes the door at the
initiation: "If you wish to only have a mundane life, then **stop working with the Quareia lessons right
here.**"[^app109] And a doctrine of **walking away from a job** runs through the initiate section.[^ini23]

**And a health check before the grade boundary — administered by the student, on the student, by
divination.** The initiate section includes a lesson called "Magical Health Check", whose instrument is three
readings of the student's own body, and whose governing sentence refuses the exclusion the name invites:

> "There is no particular health status that one needs for adept work; rather you must ensure that your body,
> mind, and energy is the best that it can be for you as an individual."[^ini88]

Nothing in it is administered by another person: a check performed on the self, with a deck, by someone who
has spent two grades learning to read the deck.

**Now the third axis, and here the blog's word does not survive contact.** The word is *gate*. What the
curriculum supplies at each grade boundary is a **self-assessment**, described in terms that forbid the
reading the blog gave it:

> "This is not a 'pass or fail' type of exam; it is a **pause-and-reflect, a self-assessment**. If you are
> being mentored then it will help your mentor understand what stage of development you are at."[^ini78]

> "There is no fail or pass with this; everyone develops in their own way. Being able to self-assess
> honestly, without fear of failure, and without grandstanding or ego, is a good skill."[^app68]

The checkpoints are therefore **not gates** in the sense the blog's table means — they are reflections, with
the student's own orientation as their purpose. That does not make them inert: a reflection set at a
specified point in a syllabus is a real structure. But the enforcement the blog claimed is not something
these lessons do. §5 takes that up.

**The checks that *are* outside the practitioner are two.** The first is the clinician, in the four-grades
form above. The second is a mentoring scheme, described as an **application** whose subject is the work
itself:

> "If you wish to be mentored through the Initiate and Adept training, these online and paper written notes
> will be a part of your application. **Without them, you cannot be mentored.**"[^adv]

Its terms are opt-in and gated: the notes are requested individually, so that no part of the work can be
skipped as unassessed, and the channel does not open early. A third has an institutional shape in the healing
module, where the course ships books to students who cannot afford them and guards the route with a real
assessment of a real claim — "if you are not known to us through mentoring or previous correspondence then we
will first **put you through an assessment** to ensure that you are, in fact, at this stage of initiate
training."[^ini81] That is as far as the outside world reaches into this curriculum, and it reaches in to
decide whether to post a book.

## 4. The counterweight: one author's system, taught by text, for a living — and where its community check sits

The counterweight goes in the body, and here it is not a hedge. It is the shape of the object.

**It is one author's system.** The volumes are by Josephine McCarthy, the copyright lines run from 1993 to
2016, and the acknowledgements name a team rather than a lineage: individuals thanked for making the course
possible, and donors.[^course] There is no founding order, no succession and no second teacher of record. The
curriculum's authority is **authorial**, not conferred — which matters, because the blog's own table wants a
*plural community* row, and the honest entry for Quareia is a single living practitioner's graded text, a
small named team, and a mentoring scheme a student has to apply for.

**It is taught by text, and its default mode is self-study.** Nothing in the scans requires a teacher: the
course is published free, the lessons address one reader, and the mid-term summary tells that reader what to
do when there is nobody:

> "So be aware that **no one is holding your hand. No one is spoon-feeding you.** You are expected to pick up
> every detail, to include the new elements for yourself, and adjust your work accordingly."[^app68]

The adept books state the same from the far end, in a lesson whose whole instrument is self-assessment:

> "If you are working with a mentor then they can go through this with you and point out weak areas… **If you
> are not being mentored and are studying the course alone** then doing this self-assessment can be a very
> good exercise as an intermediate step between theoretical study and practical application."[^ad14]

Read as a matched pair, those are the curriculum naming its own two populations: one has a mentor, one does
not, the lesson is written to work for both, and the second is the default. The mentoring is **not
supervision of the practice** but review of the record.

**Where the community check actually exists, and how narrow it is.** There is one place in fourteen volumes
where the curriculum designs an institution's safeguards rather than a student's practice, and it is not in
the course: **Appendix A of the adept Book Eleven, "The Work of the Hierophant"** — a foreword, an
introduction and twelve sections addressed to the person founding and leading a lodge. It holds the record's
only institutional language of protection, and it restrains the head of a group:

> "Keepers of the implements can be assigned and they will be responsible for the upkeep and work of the
> implement. The use of a variety of Keepers shares out the power and responsibility around the founding
> members of the lodge which is another action towards **safeguarding against power grabs and control issues
> with the Hierophant and subsequent leaders**."[^ad11]

The appendix around it is candid about the office it describes — "it is like being a parent to a lot of
people", and the "ego and greed" that end a leader's hope of "anything of true lasting worth" — and it names
the control problem in both directions: decisions taken by "people who may or may not understand the
complexities", and "the Hierophant having absolute control and then abusing it".[^ad11]

**So the blog's community row is not supplied by this curriculum to its own students.** It is supplied, in an
adept appendix, to someone about to run a lodge. For the solitary student working from the website, the
checks in the record are the sequence and its capacity thresholds, the four-grade clinical protocol, the
health-status exclusions, the self-assessments, and — if applied for — a mentor reading the journal. **The
curriculum does not claim more than that.**

**And the honest gap is where the blog must be careful.** The blog's model asks whether a frame supplies a
check that does not benefit from the answer: a reader outside the person, who can return a verdict. This
curriculum supplies one, with a clinical domain — a psychiatrist, a psychotherapist, a doctor, for the
category of problem magic does not fix — and a second whose domain is the written record, if the student
applies. For everything else — whether the work is being done, whether a vision is real, whether a key has
been found — the only reader in the record is the practitioner, and the curriculum says so: when a mentoring adept reads the notes, "if you have found a key… they will instantly
recognise it and will tell you", while the difference between a real contact and the reader's own imagination
"can be very difficult to tell for an apprentice or an initiate".[^app68]

## 5. The correction the blog owes

Two corrections running in opposite directions, which is why both need writing.

**First: the referral is stronger than the blog said, and should be carried as what it is.** [The western
column](/the-western-column/) called the doctor sentence "the single most striking passage in the Western
material" and read it as the discipline in miniature. That reading holds — and it happened without the
four-grade procedure, and without the ordering rule that makes a procedure of it. This curriculum does not
merely permit a referral: in the exorcism module it **forbids the practitioner to make the assessment**,
names the profession that must make it, insists the medical avenues have "all" been explored, orders the
clinician before the magician wherever both are needed, and instructs withdrawal where the case is full
possession. [The differential](/the-differential/) argued that the check must be external to report what the
practice cannot. **The curriculum implements that, and the blog has been carrying one sentence of it.** [The
machine has no reading](/the-machine-has-no-reading/) and [the holy daimon](/the-holy-daimon/) both
contrasted this referral against books that lack one; the contrast stands, and it is sharper than it was
drawn, because the object compared is a protocol and not a line.

**Second: "no one gets there without the previous stage having been checked" is not what the curriculum
enforces, and it is the blog's sentence rather than the course's.** The western column's §4 drew its
conclusion in two halves: the syllabus means nobody arrives at the deep state on a whim, and nobody arrives
without the previous stage having been checked. The first half is what the file supports, in the
curriculum's own words. The second is the blog's inference, and the file does not support it in that form.
**At each rung the curriculum checks the student with a self-assessment that is explicitly not
pass-or-fail**, with the mentor as an optional reader of the record; and at the grade boundary it says, in
terms, that the initiation it administers is between the practitioner and the inner world — "hence you cannot
self-initiate into an organisation" — and that what protects it is not an examiner but the keys embedded by
the work.[^app109] So the correction is a **subject change**: what the curriculum guarantees is that the
*order* is enforced and the *content* gated. The row should read: *a syllabus with a metered order, capacity
thresholds and a clinical protocol — and a check that is the practitioner's own, plus a mentor where one has
been applied for*.

**Third, a citation correction.** [The western column](/the-western-column/) sourced "to stay solid, safe and
sane… in slow, careful steps" to apprentice Module 4, Lesson 4 and used it as the curriculum's metered pace.
The address is right; the scope is wider than the use. Its subject is the **realms**, not the
coursework.[^app41] The curriculum's instruction about the *student's* pace is the advisory's first rule,
which the blog never quoted: "There is no clock ticking, and it is not a race." Both are metered; only one is
about the student's schedule, and the blog used the other one for it.

**And one scoping note the blog's own discipline requires.** The sentence the blog quotes is about **inner
hearing**, and sits in a lesson that rules out tinnitus and paranoid schizophrenia first. That is not a
narrowing of the finding; it is the finding seen properly: the referral exists here **because a differential
was built first.**

## 6. The negative finding

The series reports the presence or absence of a **safeguard**, a **metered pace** and a **clinical referral**
as search results, scoped to the file. Here the result differs from previous readings in one way that
matters: **the referral is abundant, and the blog's own word for the other two is nearly absent.**

**"Safeguard" occurs twice in the two course texts, and neither safeguards the student's mind.** In the
apprentice scan, once: a magician's "lifespan will be safeguarded" — an effect of skilled practice, in a
lesson on demons.[^app63] In the initiate scan, once: "I will put certain safeguards
into the action", of a designed exercise in which a student creates a vessel and then has to remove what
moves in — a safeguard in a **rite**, not a fence around a person.[^ini18] Across the four adept scans, twice
more: a quotation of Elizabeth I's Tilbury oration (the file quoting her), and the lodge appendix's
"safeguarding against power grabs", which protects a **group** from its own leader.[^ad11] A search shows only
what it indexes, so the claim is scoped: **the vocabulary of institutional protection appears in this
curriculum only where it is restraining a Hierophant.** Everything the blog means by "safeguard" here is
present under other names.

**There is a metered pace, and it is stated as comprehension rather than caution.** "Pace yourself" occurs
three times in the course scans;[^adv] once it is joined by a safety rationale — "for your own safety", on the
sequence fence[^app107] — and otherwise by reasons of learning. "supervisor" returns **0** hits in the course
scans, and "prerequisit" **0**.

**The clinical vocabulary is the anomaly, and it is mixed.** The counts are itemised in [^abs]; the point
here is what they are made of. "Doctor" is the commonest term by far, and nearly every instance is the text's
own narrative or a case account rather than an instruction to a reader. "Clinic" is a clinical psychologist's
research programme, "subclinical", and an acupuncture reference. **And here is the negative finding inside
the positive one:** the single occurrence of "therapist" in the apprentice scan is not a referral at all. It
is a **personality description** in the astrology lesson — "This is the therapist who is only interested in
your troubles and never discloses anything about themselves, the martyr, the Human Resources
manager…".[^app18] In the curriculum's own first module, the only therapist is a quadrant of a chart.

**And there is no exclusion statement anywhere.** "not for everyone" **0**; no list of conditions that
disqualify a person. The health lesson refuses the move outright — "There is no particular health status that
one needs for adept work" — and the sequence fence's audience is defined by work already done rather than by
capacity. The filter, by the curriculum's own account, is the student's willingness.[^app68] **That is the
shape of this protection, and it is the correction to the blog's word *gate*: the syllabus orders the
material, prints fences where the work is dangerous, and refuses to sort people.**

## 7. What a reading is for

A reading holds one source whole and asks what a blog that has quoted sources all along has to change. This
one returns a confirmation with a subject change, a strengthened instrument, and a gap.

**The confirmation is that the blog's claim survives, and that the curriculum makes it itself.** [The
western column](/the-western-column/) inferred from extracts that a curriculum is a safeguard. Read whole,
the curriculum says the same at the end of the apprentice grade — "this is how Quareia protects itself" —
with the mechanism named: the keys are embedded in the worker by the doing, and nowhere else. The blog and
the course agree about the shape of the protection. **They disagree about where it lives.** The blog's
version puts a check at each rung; the course's puts a **requirement** at each rung and a **refusal to be
skimmable** in the text, and leaves the verdict with the practitioner. That is not a weaker claim. It is a
different one, and it is more honest about its own limits than the blog was.

**The strengthened instrument is the referral.** This curriculum does not have a doctor sentence. It has a
rule about **who may assess**, one about what must be **exhausted first**, one about the **order of care**, an
instruction to **withdraw and refer** in the worst case, and a **health check performed by the practitioner
on the practitioner** at the grade boundary. The blog's uses of the single sentence are not wrong; they are
thin. The differential's boundary — some problems the practice cannot see, and the practice's duty is to know
which — is implemented here as a procedure with a scope rule.

**And the gap is the one the blog keeps returning to, arriving this time from inside a curriculum that admits
it.** Every check here that could return a verdict about the *practice* is the practitioner's own, a
documentation review by an applied-for mentor, or clinical. There is no second reader of the visions, the
contacts, the keys. [The container](/the-container/) argued that the container is the instrument doing the
auditing; [the holy daimon](/the-holy-daimon/) found a book asking its reader to judge their own state with
no other orientation. This curriculum does not ask that. It asks less, and says what it is asking: **do the
work in order, and the order will hold you up** — and where the work runs into a category it cannot touch, it
names the profession and sends the person there. The blog's table should carry that, and should stop carrying
the claim that a rung of this curriculum was checked by anyone but the person standing on it.

*Marking:* **a reading.** Documented — the curriculum's identity, structure, publication terms and every
passage in quotation marks, taken from the scans listed in [^course], checked character for character and
cited in the notes by grade, book, module, lesson and the scan's own page marker. **Address:** the scans'
line-break hyphenation is joined and their line breaks are not preserved; curly quotes and apostrophes are
rendered here as straight ASCII; the spaced en dash is rendered as an em dash; page-adjacent numerals are
dropped from quotations; where the per-book scans differ in punctuation the course scans are quoted.
**Absences reported as search results, scoped to the two course scans:** "safeguard" 1 and 1, neither about a
student; "supervisor" 0; "prerequisit" 0; "not for everyone" 0; and the clinical and pace counts itemised in
§6, terms listed in [^abs]. **Inferred, as this post's contribution:** that the curriculum's protective
mechanism is content-embeddedness rather than supervision; that its grade checkpoints are self-assessments by
