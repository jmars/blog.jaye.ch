# Black Abbot, White Magic

### The third Acher, read at its own scale — a grimoire hidden inside a cipher, an abbot who forged his own master, and the state check the manuscripts wrote, which the blog has not had before

*Fifteenth of the `readings`. [The holy daimon](/the-holy-daimon/) read Frater Acher's first volume for
a guide that is **met** and not mediated, and found no check in it but the practitioner's own judgement.
[The holy heretics](/the-holy-heretics/) read his second and found the same unmediated encounter turned
against an institution. [Geosophia](/geosophia/) read the operations underneath the Western material and
found a hazard named in order to be refused. [The western column](/the-western-column/) read the West as
a syllabus and found its safeguards written into the lessons. All four read a **meeting** or a
**curriculum**. None of them read a **text** — a book whose first subject is how a tradition hides a
practice inside another book, and whose second is what a reader has to do to get it back out.*

*Status.* This post reads **one** source, Frater Acher's *Black Abbot · White Magic*, in its Scarlet
Imprint edition, together with one external paper that the book's own footnote is out of date against.
Every quotation is verbatim, checked character for character, with the source's typographic quotation
marks rendered as straight ASCII, its small-capital drop-capital and printer's page-break artefacts
normalised, a quotation's opening capital normalised to its position in the sentence, and elisions
marked with a bare ellipsis. The reading of the material is an **inference**, offered as one. **The
book's cosmology is not endorsed** — nothing here claims that an angel, a daimon or a planetary
intelligence exists, and nothing here claims the operations do not work. No living author is diagnosed;
nothing here validates, disqualifies or restricts anyone's practice. As everywhere: describe structures,
diagnose no one.

## 1. The third Acher, and the vertex the blog left out

Two readings of this author are already here, and both concern a relation that arrives from outside and
is owned by no one. [The holy daimon](/the-holy-daimon/) reported that its daimon is met rather than
mediated, that no office stands in the route, and that the book publishes its own evidence so a reader
can judge it; [the holy heretics](/the-holy-heretics/) reported the same relation pointed at an
institution. Neither had a *document* in the middle of it.

This book puts a book where the being was. *Black Abbot · White Magic* is about Johannes Trithemius
(1462–1516), abbot of Sponheim — the "black abbot" of the title, and on Acher's account the man through
whom the medieval magical underground reached Agrippa, Dee and the early modern tradition. Its subject
is a **text**, built to be read two ways:

> "A book made from equal parts of cunning deceit and genuine magic. A book with a worldly facade, and a
> hidden chamber, patiently waiting to be discovered by a curious mind."[^book]

Every frame the blog has described as dangerous kept the check out of the participant's hands: [the
owned cosmos](/the-owned-cosmos/) hid nothing at its seat — it named the endpoint and described it
openly — and left the checks with the mediator, while [the costume](/the-costume/) and [the
certified frame](/the-certified-frame/) hid the mechanism and the warrant, so that the participant
could not see the structure at all. This is a third thing — a frame hidden from a **reader**, in which
the participant is told exactly what is happening. §4 and §9 turn on that distinction, and it is the
correction this reading owes. One more thing makes this the right third vertex: where [the holy
daimon](/the-holy-daimon/) had no clinical check at all, the manuscripts here carry an explicit rule
about the state the operator must be in *before* the work is attempted.

## 2. What the book is

*Black Abbot · White Magic: Johannes Trithemius and the Angelic Mind* is by Frater Acher, published by
Scarlet Imprint in 2020, edited by Peter Grey, designed and typeset by Alkistis Dimech, illustrated by
Jose Gabriel Alegría Sabogal, ISBN 978-1-912316-35-9.[^book] The epigraphs set the register:
Trithemius's own "Whatever in the world is knowable, I always desired to know", Adam McLean's "perhaps
the central figure in the evolution of the Western esoteric tradition", and, from D. P. Walker, the
blog's kind of sentence:

> "It cannot be proved with absolute certainty that Trithemius was in the habit of performing magical
> operations with the help of planetary angels, but it is highly probable."[^walker]

A claim measured as a probability, by a scholar, on the cover — and the book largely keeps that
standard. Acher says what kind of reader he is: "As a practicing magician, I will undertake the
challenge not through the lens of an academic scholar, but through that of a magical operator."[^intro]
The first half is **manuscripts and analysis**: translations of *Two Books · Of the knowledge and name
of one's good angel* and *The Tablet of Truth*, both attributed to a Majorcan hermit called Pelagius
Eremita, and *The General Key*, attributed to Trithemius, each with a close reading, plus *The Powder of
Pelagius* from Trithemius's own *Antipalus maleficiorum*.[^powder] The second half is biography — Klaus
Arnold's essay on Trithemius's life, then Acher on the bond between Trithemius and his supposed magical
master.[^life]

Two cautions inside the text belong in the body from the start. Acher's own, on the first page: "we
should be careful. We shall step warily into this man's past and into the maze of stories, lore and
fables he seeded for us".[^book] And one he does not soften: alongside the grimoires, the Pelagius
manuscripts and the mentoring of Agrippa, he credits Trithemius with "his role as a proponent, and
possibly an instigator of the witch hunts in the 16th and 17th century".[^master] A book that reports
its subject's part in the trials has its counterweight already in the body, and that one is the book's,
not this post's.

## 3. The double text

Now the subject the blog cannot afford to blur: what *Steganographia* is. In 1499 Trithemius wrote to a
Carmelite friend, Arnold Bostius, describing a book he was finishing; Bostius died before the letter
arrived, the prior of Ghent opened it, and copies went across Europe.[^life] In it Trithemius claims,
for the second book, an art of conveying "my thoughts to the initiate over any kind of distance …
without words, writing or signs by whatever kind of messenger", such that a messenger "pressed for an
answer by torture … would not be able to confess anything of the message which is entirely unknown to
him" — adding that all of it can be done "without the help of superstitious means and without the
assistance of spirits".[^life] Read that last clause again: the book on angel magic is announced, by its
author, as not needing angels.

Walker's *Spiritual and Demonic Magic* (1958) — the same Walker as the epigraph — established the
reading the book's own footnote still follows: the first two books of the *Steganographia* are a
cryptographic system wearing the costume of a grimoire, and only the unfinished third is the magic it
appears to be. (The whole-book version of the cipher reading, all three at once, is Wayne Shumaker's.)
Acher accepts the double reading and goes past it:

> "The most likely answer is both. Trithemius' *Steganographia* is partly a treatise on cryptography in
> which the methods of encipherment are disguised as demonic magic and partly a treatise on demonic
> magic."[^life]

The book does not choose, and it gives the reason: Trithemius "deliberately chose the most infamous and
dangerous genre of his time in which to conceal his message", when he could have hidden the same keys in
a poem or a chronicle.[^life]

**And here the book is out of date in its own footnote, which the post has to say.** Its note runs:

> "To be precise, most researchers today agree that the first two books of the *Steganographia* contain
> cryptographic instructions in disguise; it is the unfinished third book, however, that stands out as
> it does not seem to contain cryptographic code, yet rather concentrates on the actual angelic magic
> described in it in detail, e.g. Walker: 87".[^life]

That is Walker's position, cited to Walker: the state of the question as of 1958, with Walker's third
book the real grimoire. It is no longer that. Two cryptologists solved the third book — Thomas Ernst in
*Daphnis* in 1996, and Jim Reeds in *Cryptologia* in 1998, whose abstract reads in full:

> "Book III of Trithemius's *Steganographia* (written ca. 1500) contains hidden cipher messages within
> what is ostensibly a work on magic. After almost 500 years these cryptograms have been detected and
> solved. (Since 1606 it was known that similar ciphers were present in Books I and II.) As a result
> the *Steganographia* can no longer be regarded as one of the main early modern demonological treatises
> but instead stands unambiguously revealed as the first book-length treatment of cryptography in
> Europe."[^reeds]

So the correction runs one line further than the book, in the direction the book was already going: its
thesis is that the frame can be read two ways, and the scholarship since 1996 says Book III is a third
cipher too. The reading did not collapse to "only a cipher" — the text still tells a practitioner
exactly how to do the operation — but the *proportion* of the double reading moves. Acher's "most likely
answer is both" survives on the broader text; his aside that the third book escapes the cipher does not.
The blog reports both: established, and argued.

## 4. Hidden in plain sight

The phrase the book uses is the phrase to keep. Acher, on what Trithemius knew how to do:

> "As a life-long reformer of magic and master cryptographer we can surmise that he would have known how
> to conceal in plain sight a heretical teaching in the periphery of a work focussed on a much more
> straightforward operation."[^tablet-analysis]

The book also gives the prosecutor's frame verbatim. In 1504 the respected Parisian theologian Carolus
Bovillus spent two weeks at Sponheim, was shown the manuscript but not the key to it, and then wrote to a
bishop:

> "I took myself to Trithemius; who I encountered entirely as a sorcerer and in no manner whatsoever a
> philosopher."[^life]

And then: "if the good angel of the Lord still exists, as I believe, so may he cut him in two, right
down the middle and resolve the wicked pact he had formed with the evil angels."[^life] That is a frame
supplied from outside by a reader who did not perform the operation and could not read the text. The
apparatus of "white magic" in the title is an attempt to write a magic that could survive him:
Trithemius's project was to "reintroduce certain forms of magic into the curriculum of the Christian
Church", knowing the alternative was "to not end on the pyre".[^intro] When the work is legible as
sorcery, the practitioner's problem is not whether the daimon is real but whether the man holding the
book will be believed.

## 5. The angelic mind, in the manuscripts' own words

Acher's term for Trithemius's target is the **angelic mind**, and the goal is stated in the Introduction
in a sentence worth reading slowly: "the one essential goal of all white magic: to make the human and
the angelic minds alike."[^intro] The manuscripts put it in the operative voice. From the first chapter
of Pelagius's *Two Books*:

> "But if man wants to achieve this, he must unite himself with the angelic mind and become
> alike."[^twobooks]

The two minds differ from the outset: "the angelic understanding is complete in all parts", while "man
with the help of the angel can attain a knowledge of all future and present things".[^twobooks] Acher
says the instruction deliberately de-personalises the being: Pelagius "deliberately uses the
non-individual form when referring to the angelic mind", evoking "the idea of an angelic hive-mind, or
at least the notion that all angels can access the same reservoir of perfected
knowledge".[^twobooks-analysis] Set that beside [the holy daimon](/the-holy-daimon/), where the being
was named, personal and met, and the emphasis has moved: what is to be joined is a *body of knowledge
with a mind attached*, and the operation is first of all an act of **assimilation**. Acher is explicit
that this is load-bearing: "The key to the operation lies in the gradual assimilation of the human mind
to the angelic mind until they have become sufficiently alike that they can hold a shared
space."[^twobooks-analysis] And the manuscripts put the life first: "all of these ritual preparations,
prayers and acts of devotion will be null and void if the practitioner has not adopted a way of life
that allows the angelic mind to come close to and thence continually dwell in proximity to their human
mind."[^twobooks-analysis] That is the finding [the holy daimon](/the-holy-daimon/) reported from the
other direction — the work on the self is the prerequisite, and the encounter happens on the way out —
stated here as arithmetic: a life, or nothing.

**And here is the counterweight, in the body, and it is the book's own.** Of the channel through
thoughts — the third of the three Acher identifies, alongside dreams and visible appearance — he names
the obvious objection and dissolves it:

> "The Achilles' heel of this method might seem to be our human (in)ability to differentiate between our
> own thoughts and the ones received through angelic mediation." "However, if looked at through a more
> practical lens, this concern turns out to be void: to the person with a problem it doesn't matter
> where the solution comes from — either from their own ingenious free spirit or through the mediation
> of another being — as long as it solves the problem."[^twobooks-analysis]

The blog's whole instrument set — [the differential](/the-differential/), [the machine said
so](/the-machine-said-so/), [the label](/the-label/) — exists because that indistinguishability is the
problem, not a technicality: a source trusted as external is a source that can no longer be checked.
This is the book saying the opposite in one clause — origin is dropped for result — and the two criteria
are not reconcilable: if the criterion is *it worked*, there is nothing left to check.

## 6. What the manuscripts actually prescribe

The blog's daimon readings have had no practical layer at source; Acher's are translations of
operations, so it is worth stating plainly what they ask for.

**The *Two Books* is dream incubation, Christianly framed.** Book I gives nineteen chapters: live alone
and chastely, pray without ceasing, know the star and hour of your angel, and put the question to God so
that it can be answered "in my sleep through your holy angel N.N."[^twobooks-ritual] Book II is the
consecration — a clean chamber with a crucifix and three wax candles consecrated on a Marian feast, a
written question on a consecrated wax tablet placed under the pillow, a confession, and then a long body
of orisons addressed in turn to Father, Son, Holy Spirit, the Virgin, and the angel, named by the
practitioner as "N.N."[^twobooks-ritual] At the close the practitioner faces the four quarters and
speaks an exorcism — "Begone, therefore, far from here, in the name of the Father and the Son and the
Holy Spirit!" — and then goes to sleep "without having spoken to anyone and not having taken up any
other matter in your thoughts".[^twobooks-ritual] **The *Tablet of Truth* is a pendulum operation**, the
most concretely magical thing in the book. Attributed to Pelagius and transmitted through the
intermediary Libanius Gallus, its title names its angel: "The Work of ALMIEL. The Tablet of Truth …
under the office and direction of the holy angel ALMIEL, who reveals the truth in dubious and ambiguous
matters", with the manuscript's gloss that "Almiel is a peaceful angel and friend of life, who abhors
the shedding of blood".[^tablet] The operator makes a wooden tablet with a design on its face and a
conjuration circle on its reverse, has it consecrated by a priest, seals a splinter of the True Cross
into a cavity in it, and suspends a needle threaded on silk, stuck into a consecrated piece of bread,
over a cross. A movement east–west is yes; north–south is no. The angel moves the
bread.[^tablet-analysis] That is the operation in full, and it is a *technique* — a divinatory
instrument with a hinge, a consecration and a rule for reading it, in the same family as the
ring-over-a-plate of the witch trials that Acher traces back to Ammianus.[^tablet-analysis] It is
described apparatus, with a named agent and a reading rule.

**The *General Key* is a grimoire of sympathetic magic**, spurious to Trithemius — his own *General Key*
was the cryptographic key to the *Steganographia*, a different book entirely — and Acher is clear that
the attribution is false while defending why the text is worth reading: it condenses the premises of
astral magic into numbered rules and then performs one full example.[^generalkey] The example is the one
the post has to name, because it is the in-body counterweight to the phrase "white magic". It is a love
philtre: a dove killed and its heart burned while still quivering; powders mixed with the operator's own
blood; a parchment figure written with two names joined by the angel's name and burnt; and the result a
powder "which if drunk to the health of the person [will ensure] that person will be in love with you
incessantly until their death".[^generalkey] The prayer over the incense asks the angel to bless it "so
that it possesses the power to bind all women in love to me".[^generalkey] A book whose title announces
a purified white magic contains, in the text it publishes, a recipe for drugging a third party into
love. Acher reads the animal sacrifice level-headedly and does not pretend the operation is anything
other than what it is.[^generalkey] The boundary between white and black here is drawn by the *intent of
the operator*, and the same manual contains both registers.

Two boundaries in that manual belong in the body too. Rule 7 of the *General Key*'s twelve, after the
instruction that everything used must be unused, reads: "These things shall not be seen or touched by a
woman or a maiden who is menstruating, otherwise they lose their power."[^generalkey] And the *Two
Books* bars from the practice "heretics, infidel Christians, those who lead a swinish life", running on
through gluttons, boozers, the unchaste, mockers, bandits, murderers, thieves, liars, gamblers,
"dalliers", adulterers and "black artists": "all are incapable and unworthy to possess knowledge of
this."[^twobooks] A tradition of white magic that defines itself by what it excludes has told you
something about itself, and it is not the daimon.

## 7. The caution, searched and reported

The vocabulary the blog has used before — safeguard, caution, danger, pace, warning, protection — was
looked for across the whole book. **The word "safeguard" occurs once, and not as a warning**: it is in
the fifth prayer of the *Tablet of Truth*'s consecration, to the guardian angel, who "guards the soul of
this servant of God and safeguards it on every path".[^tablet] There is no "physician", no
"psychiatrist", no "mental health", no "sanity", and no referral of any kind to anyone outside the
practice; "doctor" appears three times and every one means a learned man.[^twobooks] The **clinical
referral is absent**, which means the second Acher reading in this series has found the same absence in
the same author. A search shows only what it indexes, so the claim is scoped: within this book, the
referral is not there.

**But the operational caution is not absent, and it is stronger here than in *Holy Daimon*.** It comes
in three forms, each inside the operation.

**One — a state check before the work, and it is explicit:**

> "Additionally, you should take care not to attempt to inquire in pursuit of the truth by means of this
> tablet, in cases when you are not in a good mental state, if your head is not right, if you are not
> well disposed, that is, in cases where you are sad, melancholic or generally confused, when you are
> tired, lethargic, scowling, when you have overly indulged in wine or food or are in disorder in any
> other way, because under these circumstances you will not have success in your work, but rather will
> experience illusion."[^tablet-rules]

This is the instrument the blog has been asking about since [the differential](/the-differential/): a
rule that says *do not operate in this state*. It is a self-check and not a referral — the remedy for a
bad state is to postpone the operation, not to leave the tradition — but it is written down, at source,
for the moment before the work starts.

**Two — a refusal list.** The *Tablet*'s rule 5: "Never put this tablet to use for the killing of a
human being, for theft, robbery or any other crime by which the respective person might be killed, for
the spirit ALMIEL and his companions despise bloodshed amongst humans."[^tablet-rules] The *Two Books*
has the same structure without the tablet, and goes further than a modern reader will expect: "Also
based upon such insights and received revelations nobody should be convicted or sentenced to death", and
it limits legitimate questions to "honest, befitting things such as about your health, food,
wealth".[^twobooks] A divinatory technique that can give a true answer is a technique that can kill
someone by accusation, and the manuscript closes that door in its own list of rules.

**Three — an oath sworn to a priest, and it holds the closest thing in this book to a clinical
sentence.** Before the consecrated tablet is handed over, the priest puts a set of questions to the
operator:

> "Will you act in each operation with maturity? That is, you will not perform the action out of
> recklessness, on a childish inclination, or as a confused or insane soul?"[^tablet]

The operator swears, gives his hand on it, confesses and communicates. The check on the person is not a
diagnosis and not a screening; it is a vow taken in front of another man, and the other man is the
priest who has just been told, in the same manuscript, to be careful that he himself is no mocker, since
a mocker's consecration "will be void".[^tablet] There is a *witness* in the operation — the only
witness in the whole book — and he is there to refuse it to someone who is not in a position to use it.

**And the danger the book names most often is not a danger to the practitioner at all.** It is the
danger of being read as a sorcerer: the *Steganographia*'s "risk of being construed as demonic
magic",[^life] and the observation that "such practices could easily land one on the
pyre".[^tablet-analysis]

The negative finding, stated plainly: **this book carries no clinical referral, and the check it does
carry is a state check plus a refusal list plus an oath — all of them inside the operation and none of
them outside the tradition.** For the blog's count: Acher's first volume has no check, Quareia's has a
clinical triage and a referral out, and this is the middle case — a *gate on the state of the operator*,
with no door in it leading out of the practice.

## 8. The master who never existed

The whole practical layer of §6 is attributed to Pelagius Eremita, and Acher argues — flatly, in his own
voice, with the evidence set out — that Pelagius never lived:

> "not only is Trithemius the only confirmed literary source we have for the works of this 15th century
> hermit, but in fact Pelagius Eremita never existed." "The only conclusion to draw is that Trithemius
> invented this pen name in order to share his own magico-mystical teachings under the secure disguise
> of an occult authority."[^forgery]

His evidence is documentary and checkable. The *Two Books* cites Agrippa — "as Cornelius Agrippa writes
in his *Philosophia* about these things" — and *De occulta philosophia* did not exist in manuscript
before 1510, so the self-dating to 1480 "is obviously false".[^forgery] Every mention of Pelagius and
his messenger Libanius Gallus comes from Trithemius's own letters and books, and only his; Carlos Gilly
calls one of the letters "the freely invented purported letter which Trithemius' alleged teacher
Libanius Gallus wrote on 6.4.1499", a fiction that "has deceived scientists until today".[^forgery]
Acher places the invention in 1505–1509, after the leaked letter and after Bovillus — and he names the
precedent, in a sentence the blog should quote because it is an author describing his own tradition's
method without flinching:

> "Overstepping and deliberately blurring the lines between fact, fiction and forgery, is at the heart
> of the Western magical tradition, whether one appreciates or is appalled by it."[^forgery]

**Now the blog's part, and it has to be stated without tidying.** Every one of the operations in §6 is a
text Acher translates in full and recommends to a modern practitioner as "a wealth of relevant material
for the modern practitioner" — and the argument of the second half is that the man who wrote it was the
abbot whose name is on the spine of the book.[^twobooks-analysis] That is not a debunking: on his
account the material is *stronger*, not weaker, for having an author. The blog's job is narrower. It is
to note that the material offers no independent witness either — the text's authority derives from a
master, the master was invented by the author, and the author is the only source. [The follower
post](/the-follower/) and [the empty vessel](/the-empty-vessel/) both dealt with authority routed
through an absent person; here the routing is deliberate and literary, and the person is a pen name. On
the book's own argument these texts are not a surviving record of a fifteenth-century tradition but the
composition of one man, published after his own attempt to write in his own name went badly wrong. That
is a finding about a text's provenance, not about anyone's experience of it: nothing here says the
operation is inert.

## 9. The correction the blog owes

Three readings of this author, and this one has moved the register twice.

**The correction, stated explicitly: the blog's question has been where the check is held, and this book
moves it to who the concealment is aimed at.** The frames the blog has analysed as dangerous each put
the check somewhere other than the participant's hands. [The owned cosmos](/the-owned-cosmos/) is the
cleanest case, because it hides nothing at all: it names the endpoint and describes the seat openly —
"the cosmology did not merely permit it. The cosmology described it" — and what makes it the most
dangerous of the three is that its checks are "held by the mediator". [The costume](/the-costume/) hides
the mechanism and [the certified frame](/the-certified-frame/) hides the warrant, and there too the
participant is the one who cannot verify what is being done to them. So the axis is not hidden versus
open but **where the check is held**. This book adds a second axis: it is about a frame hidden *from an
adversary*, where the participant is told everything. Trithemius writes an operation in full, hides it
inside a text that reads as demonic magic, and the reader he is hiding it from is the reader who would
burn him: the concealment protects the practitioner from the prosecutor, and the practitioner is given
the technique entire. Acher's phrase is that a master cryptographer "would have known how to conceal in
plain sight a heretical teaching", and the record shows why he had to — Bovillus got through only a few
chapter openings and did not hold the book for longer than two hours, and wrote that the man was a
sorcerer, and that verdict came close to finishing him. The blog owes the distinction because the two
look identical from the outside — a text that is not telling you what it is — and because the blog's
existing rule, *a frame that hides its structure is doing work on the person inside it*, is false of
this one. The rule needs an addition, not a retraction: **the blog must ask who the concealment is
aimed at.** Concealment aimed at the occupant is the mechanism; concealment aimed at a third party is a
defence, and the tell is whether the participant knows the shape of the operation. Here they do. That
correction has a cost: if the same text is legitimately legible as a cipher and as a grimoire, the
reading the blog brings is not neutral — it is the same act the prosecutor performed, at the same text,
with a different verdict, which is what [the machine has no reading](/the-machine-has-no-reading/) says,
arrived at from inside the tradition.

**A second correction, smaller and narrower, and the triangle closes on it.** §5 raised the question of
whether Acher's "angelic mind" is a **seat** — an owned endpoint with a person at it — or the lateral
guide of [the holy daimon](/the-holy-daimon/). The book answers it in its own words, in the passage
where Acher describes what the invention was *for*:

> "No religious community is necessary to forge this bond. With Trithemius the bond becomes the direct
> and personal relationship of every man and their angel. And with this final elitist turn, all
> responsibility rests entirely with each individual."[^master]

And in the same analysis: "In stark contrast to the Solomonic tradition, what is central to Trithemius'
tradition of white magic is not an act of demonic command and coercion, but a continuous effort from the
human side towards angelic assimilation."[^master] A bond that needs no community, direct and personal,
whose whole responsibility sits with the individual, achieved by assimilation rather than command: that
is the relation [the holy daimon](/the-holy-daimon/) found, and Acher says so with the word — he calls
the Majorcan hermit's being the "holy daimon". **The blog's triangle is therefore not three kinds of
relation. It is one relation — unmediated, unowned, outward — met three times: as a guide, against an
institution, and behind a text.** The correction is that the blog must stop treating the text as a
*mediator*: it is not what stands between the practitioner and the encounter, but one of the places the
encounter has been stored.

**And with that, the counterweight, because the triangular reading must not be allowed to make the book
tidy.** The relation may be the same. The **route** is not. The *Tablet of Truth* cannot be started
without a priest: he consecrates the tablet, blesses the needle and the thread and the bread and the
wax, says three masses, hears the operator's confession, and hands the finished instrument over with the
words that grant "rulership over the spirits".[^tablet] The book is kept secret under an oath sworn to
that priest, and the manuscript's closing instruction is that the work is not to be shown "to a person
who is unlike us, for any cause, prize, or reward".[^tablet] So the relation is unmediated and the
*access* is gated — by a cleric, by an oath, and by an instrument only a priest can commission. That is
not [the western column](/the-western-column/)'s graded curriculum, where the gate is a syllabus: this
gate is not a stage on a path, it is a permission. The earlier finding was that the West wrote its
safeguards into the syllabus; the addition is that this corner of the West also wrote its *practice*
into a lock, and gave the key to a man in a stole.

## 10. What this reading adds

Two things, briefly. **One — the caution is real, and it is not clinical.** §7 found a state check, a
refusal list and an oath, and no referral, which makes the clinical referral look like what it is: a
modern addition, bolted on where a tradition had a gate but no door. The manuscript's answer to a
practitioner in a bad state is *postpone the operation*; [the safeguards](/safeguards/) and the clinical
column say the answer is *leave the practice and see someone*. Both are written down, and they are not
the same instrument.

**Two — the two-way frame.** The blog has spent a great deal of space on frames hidden from the people
inside them. This is the first source that shows the opposite case from within: an author who codes his
own work, the prosecutor who reads it the other way, and the reader whose method is to hold both
readings at once and refuse to collapse them. "The most likely answer is both" is the book's sentence
about the *Steganographia*, and it is the honest sentence to end on, because the post had to add a
correction to it: the cryptologists who came after the book's sources solved the third book, and the
cipher did not stop at two.

What [the holy daimon](/the-holy-daimon/) called the check that was missing, this book has partly
supplied and partly left where it was. The manuscripts write down when not to work. They do not write
down who to tell when the work has gone wrong. And the practice they describe is not owned by a seat and
not mediated by an institution; it is mediated by a man in a stole who holds the tablet, and it is
hidden from a reader who would kill you. That is not the blog's cosmology and it is not the blog's
permission. It is one text, read at its own scale, and the frame in it is one the blog had not learned
to ask about: **not who is being hidden from — who is being hidden from whom.**
