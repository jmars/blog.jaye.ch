# Ani.Mystic

### One book, read at its own scale — a cosmos of persons, an epistemology whose check is participation, and the frame the blog's own guardrail was written for

*Fifth of the `readings`. [The frame is a variable](/the-frame-is-a-variable/) opened the frames series with this book —
Hemingway's bankruptcy, "the disastrous metaphor of the universe as a machine", and the criticism of a regimen
treated as "optional ambiance"; [a cosmos of persons](/a-cosmos-of-persons/) built its safeguard on White's account
of Pohnpei, where everything is made of the same "stuff" which is a kind of personhood. **This post reads the book
those posts were quoting.** It repeats nothing of them: their business was the levers, and this post's is the book
whole — including the part a quotation cannot carry, which is what the book says knowledge **is**. [The machine has
no reading](/the-machine-has-no-reading/) and [the cosmos that refuses](/the-cosmos-that-refuses/) supply the
objection this reading has to hold beside it, and [the owned cosmos](/the-owned-cosmos/) supplies the one
comparison that matters, because a frame with an aim and no seat is not the same object as a frame with a person
standing at the end of the road.*

*Status.* This post reads **one** source. The quotations are verbatim from the digital text, checked character for
character against it and cited by chapter in the notes — by chapter and not by page, because the text's
sections stand out of book order and its running heads carry no numbers; the reading of them is an **inference**, offered as
one. **The book's cosmology is not endorsed** — nothing here claims the cosmos is alive and nothing claims it is
not, and §6 and §9 are about why holding that open is the whole of the discipline here. This is the book the
blog's own guardrail was written for, and the guardrail is held throughout: **Gordon White is cited for what he
argues, never adopted as the frame.** No living author is diagnosed. As everywhere: describe structures, diagnose
no one.

---

## 1. The book the blog has been quoting

When the frames series was built, it wrote itself a rule and wrote down why:

> **Ani.Mystic is an argument FOR a cosmology. Hold White the way the traditions are held — as a source ABOUT
> frames, never as the frame. The tell is his own: the danger is not a cosmology, it is one that cannot be
> checked from outside.**

The two posts that used him did exactly that. [The opener](/the-frame-is-a-variable/) took two quotations — the
machine metaphor's arrival, and the personhood passage — and read them structurally, as evidence for a claim about
frames. [A cosmos of persons](/a-cosmos-of-persons/) took the second further and drew the safeguard out of it. Both
were right to. Both were also **selection**: a source quoted for the sentence that fits a thesis has been reduced to
that sentence, and what is left off the page is the book's own argument about how it should be read.

That is the gap this reading closes, and what is left out is the largest thing in the book. This is not primarily a
description of an enchanted cosmos. It is an **argument about how to know**, made from inside such a cosmos,
addressed to readers who are not in one. So: the book at its own scale; its epistemology as the book states it,
which is the most contestable thing in it; and then the blog's objection, unresolved — because the objection is
[the cosmos that refuses](/the-cosmos-that-refuses/)' finding, that a safeguard can be **claimed**, arriving one
scale up at a frame that need not even be claimed. Only practised.

## 2. What the book is, in the author's own terms

*Ani.Mystic: Encounters with a Living Cosmos* is by Gordon White, published by Scarlet Imprint, and its contents
page is an argument in miniature: an Introduction, "Island of my Grandfather"; seven chapters of travel writing,
ethnography and magical memoir — "The Altar in the Middle of the Ocean", "Whetu Ariki", "Sky Country", "The Farm at
the End of the World" among them; and then, in place of a bibliography, a final chapter called "Book People".[^am]

The book gives two definitions of animism and marks the second as its own. The first is the working one — "animism
is the belief that the world is made up of persons, only some of whom are humans. These personhoods are
constituted relationally with other persons, and it is the business — busy-ness — of the animist to ensure and
promote right relations."[^anim] The second is the author's, and it is stranger than a definition of belief:

> "animism describes a living cosmos incorporating an immanent spirit world, of multiple epistemologies operating
> in frictionless and non-extractive ways, in which beings are relationally composed toward the cosmic aim of
> mutual flourishing."[^anim]

Read that twice: it names a **substrate**, makes a claim about **knowledge** (multiple epistemologies, operating
non-extractively), and names an **aim** (mutual flourishing). §5 takes the second clause and §7 the third.

The opposing term is the book's too. **Materialist-naturalism** is "the premise that only physical things exist and
that all events — including human thought — are as a result of material processes", joined to the premise "that the
universe operates according to blind forces and natural laws", yielding "the predominant so-called western view of
the universe as a place devoid of purpose, in which impersonal, often random, forces impact a universe composed
exclusively of dead matter".[^anim] And the goal is stated with the author's characteristic wit: to examine "what
the so-called western magical arts … look, feel and are like when situated in a fully-aware, animist epistemology
and not the **monstrous octoparrot** of consumerist materialist-naturalism".[^anim]

**So the shape of the object is not the shape the quotes implied.** Not a defence of magic, and not a survey of
animism — an argument that the western magical arts have been practised for two centuries inside a cosmology that
cannot support them, and that the cosmology is what has to change. Which makes the epistemology, not the
enchantment, load-bearing. And one further thing is load-bearing: **the book is written from inside, on purpose.**
White tells the reader he is "not an academic" but that the book "must explicitly be a magician's book", and that the works in that literature
"… carry the telos of field work, of going out from the academy to examine the world, and then reporting back to
the academy". A magician's book "is not derived from the direction of *going out into*, but of *going about
the world*, going about the business of magic".[^mag]

## 3. The diagnosis: the disastrous metaphor, and how it arrived

The diagnosis of why the West cannot see a living cosmos is concentrated in the first chapter, and it opens
literarily. There is "that famous line of Hemingway's from *The Sun Also Rises*":

> "'How did you go bankrupt?' 'Two ways. Gradually, then suddenly.' He could also be describing how the west came
> by the **disastrous metaphor of the universe as a machine.**"[^machine]

The chapter then does the work of the adverb: "gradually" runs from Plato's dualist epistemology and the
Neoplatonists through a Christian cosmology to the forerunners of the Enlightenment, and "suddenly" is the
Enlightenment proper. White follows Tarnas's *The Passion of the Western Mind* through three turning points — the
Copernican model, the "cosmological estrangement"; Descartes, the "ontological estrangement"; and Kant, "the final
turning point that completes our estrangement", the "epistemological estrangement".[^machine]

What the sequence produced is a specific incompetence, and its name is **explanationism**: "Explanationism and the
removal of all agency from the cosmos can only have arisen hand-in-hand", and what was lost is "the restoration of
agency — a wholly serviceable definition of animism in and of itself", set against "what Charles Fort called the
epistemology of explanation".[^machine] The consequence is stated as bluntly as a title: "we live in a dead,
meaningless, clockwork universe."[^machine] And the consequence of *that* is what the frames posts drew on — two
universes, and a failure of perception rather than of theory:

> "The conflict was emblematic of the **formed universe of the machine metaphor failing to perceive the
> perpetually-forming universe of the living one.**"[^machine]

From there the book's positive claim is one sentence long, and it declares its own epistemic status inside the
sentence: Latour's "we have never been modern" is "the good news, as it should make it easier to leave the dominant
metaphor of cosmos as machine for the far more **medicinal and probably true** metaphor of the universe as living
being."[^machine] *Probably true.* That is the register the book argues in.

**The counterweight, in the body, because it belongs here.** This is intellectual history with a verdict attached,
and the verdict is not the blog's. [The machine frame](/the-machine-has-no-reading/) has already had its due in
this blog: it is the frame that made auditable measurement possible — the frame this blog is written in — and its
blindness to a person's interior is a **blind spot, not a lie**. What the chapter establishes, at the level this
series cares about, is what the frames posts found: **the machine metaphor does not present as a metaphor.** It
presents as the universe described without adornment.

## 4. The epistemology, first half: what a book is for

Now the part quoting could not reach. The book's most contestable material is not about spirits. It is about
validation, and it sits in the front and back matter — the Introduction's sections "A Magician's Book" and "A
Coming to Terms", and the final chapter "Book People", whose first paragraph is this:

> "To finish a book like this with a typical bibliography feels almost like a betrayal. Why spend so many pages
> exploring **methods of truth validation better suited to a forming universe** if, in the end, I was going to rely
> on the authority of a book pile from a **formed one**? And why do we have bibliographies, anyway? You may say it
> is so interested readers can move further into a topic but I think it is so we can play **gotcha** with an
> author."[^bp]

A little further on: "Bibliographies are not about the books that are in them but the ones that are not."[^bp] The
replacement is announced in the Introduction: "in lieu of a bibliography, please find instead a final chapter, Book
People, which details not only the books that contributed 'ideas' to this book, but also the ideas themselves."[^bp]

**The demand under critique is not reading** — White is proud of the tradition's literacy. What is under critique
is that a **claim be validated by pointing at other validated texts**, and its two exponents are the gotcha (the
interrogative use, where a source is a weapon) and the book pile (the citational use, where a source is a
credential). Against both, "Book People" offers the source as a **companion** — here is the thought, here is where
I found it, here is what it did for me — a bibliography with the validation stripped out and the encounter left
in.[^bp]

The Introduction is sharper, making the same argument at the level of the sentence: the academy's citational form
is a **survival of a theological one** — "This is true because I found it in that authoritative book", Church
Fathers citing Gospels, papers citing papers. The test it fails is concrete:

> "You still cannot put in your footnote 'I learned this from a tree' or 'my grandmother told me this in dream'
> because these are not legitimate sources that can go into building your text-based truth claim."[^mag]

Then the positive account, which is the epistemology proper: "it is to reconfigure one's understanding of
'knowledge' as an **encounter**"; the words are "encountered in an unavoidable context. The ambient temperature.
The feel of the book in your hands. […] What you can hear."[^mag] Hence the book on itself: "So this is not an 'object'
in which I present my 'thoughts' for you to consume or reject, but rather an opportunity to encounter and follow my
thinking and those thinkings that I myself found and followed."[^mag] And hence its instruction to the reader:
"Read the book in multiple locations. Wild places. Urban places. […] Read it aloud to your ancestors."[^mag]

The book even names the alternative, and the name is the best thing in the Introduction. Yolngu elders, handing
their knowledge to a university curriculum, made it a condition that the educators teach the students "secret
English" — "the words used to make something Officially True within a colonial overculture. Words that determine
what is and is not allowed to exist." And the sentence that matters for §6: "Secret English is not learning
different words for the same thing. It comes with a **unidirectional method of truth validation** that many of us
mistake for reality."[^anim] That is a real claim, stated with unusual candour, and note what it is *not*: not that
texts are bad, or evidence, or that the West has no knowledge — but that a method of validation can be **one-way**,
and be mistaken for a description of reality by the people who use it. The objection comes in §6 and is not trivial;
but the claim has to be reported as made, and it is made by an author who also hopes his own book will be "an
authoritative one — on how books and the things we put in them … do not have sole claim to authority".[^mag]

## 5. The epistemology, second half: what the book says the check is

If §4 is the criticism, §5 is the alternative — and here the reading has to be exact, because the book's answer to
*how do you know* is not a shrug and not "believe it". **The answer is participation.**

The clearest statement is in the fifth chapter, under a heading that is the whole answer in two words:

> "Twentieth century Italian anthropologist, Ernesto de Martino, observed that cultures with a stronger belief in
> magic tend to see more spectacular magical results. […] **Doing it makes it work better, and when it works
> better then more data become usefully available, which makes it work better.**"[^check]

That is a feedback loop, and the book is explicit that it is the mechanism of validation. The same claim returns
as a comparative finding, stated as an empirical result: a spirit-centred framework "is not only how most humans
appear to experience reality, **it also works better, regardless of the beliefs of participating humans.** Getting
right with the Dead makes the world better."[^check] The evidence offered is the parapsychological and
healing-and-intention literature, cited as data.[^check]

The book's name for the era it thinks we are in is Charles Fort's. Fort's successive "Dominants", each defined by
"the way in which it makes things true", run: "The Dominant of Religion had an epistemology of **belief**. The
Dominant of Science had an epistemology of **explanation**. The Dominant of Intermediatism has an epistemology of
**expression and acceptance**" — and the reason for the third is the book's thesis about the machine frame,
compressed: "Explanations belong in the machine metaphor."[^check]

Two pieces complete the account. The first is a name for the method — "'continual contact as epistemology'", the
book's term for a mode in which ceremony, two-way thinking and synchronicity are not moods but a theory of mind:
"Animisms rely on contingent knowings, frameworks of understanding that allow for the universe to be alive and
transforming, not fixed or complete", and "it is how one **worlds with a world that is perpetually forming rather
than already formed**."[^check] The second is wayfaring: knowledge generated *with and moving through* place rather
than tabulated in fixed points, which White extends into Micronesian and Polynesian navigation, where the canoe is
stationary and the cosmos moves.[^way] The book's summary of what it is looking for is in its last chapter, and it
is the sentence this section turns on:

> "**How we encounter and validate without invalidating truths** is where we turn next, and Ayahuasca is the guide
> and yardstick."[^way]

**Read the check out of that, because it is the finding.** The book does not say the cosmos is alive and therefore
anything goes. It says there is a **way of knowing** suited to a forming universe, and that the way is:
participate — practise, keep contact, keep the enquiry open, notice what it produces — and let the practising be
the validation. It is not credulity: it has a slogan, a law and a named era. But note the method's instrument. In
Ernesto's Law the practising **is** the data channel; the loop that validates is the loop that practises. Which
leaves the book's final instruction to the reader, in a footnote at the end of the last chapter: "This is the last
chapter of the book, so hopefully by now you have a **healthier framework with which to validate your
experiences**."[^check] The framework validates the experiences. That sentence is the bridge to §6.

## 6. The objection, stated exactly

The blog's test is not *is this frame true*. It is: **can the frame be checked by someone who does not benefit?**
And the blog has run it at this scale four times. [The machine has no reading](/the-machine-has-no-reading/) found
that a frame with no category for an experience **cedes the reading to whoever supplies one**. [The cosmos that
refuses](/the-cosmos-that-refuses/) found the apophatic refusal is a safeguard that **can be claimed**: "I cannot
describe what I have seen" is not humble in the wrong mouth, it is the strongest claim available. [The owned
cosmos](/the-owned-cosmos/) found a frame with a seat **converts every test into another credential**. And the
series' result was that **no property of a cosmology is self-enforcing**.

Now put Ani.Mystic in that row. The guardrail says: "the danger is not a cosmology, it is one that cannot be checked
from outside." **This book's frame cannot be checked from outside, and the book says so as a virtue** — in the words
that close the Introduction's argument about books: "**There is no 'outside' the book, nor should there be.**"[^mag]
Follow the claim to its end and it is general: if knowledge is an encounter and the world is a participant, then a
demand for a check that stands outside the encounter is precisely the demand of the epistemology the Introduction
has dismantled. Secret English's "unidirectional method of truth validation" *is* the outside check. Declining it is
not a gap in the book's account. It is the book's position.

**So White's answer to the blog's objection is a real answer, and this is it:** the cosmos is **participatory**,
so externality is the wrong requirement. A living cosmos has no outside, because there is nowhere outside it to
stand; the moss, the pig, the compost, the dead and the researcher are all parties, and a test that requires a
non-participant asks for a position the frame says does not exist. Validation in such a cosmos is contact, kept up
over time, checked by what the contact produces — not evasion, but the strongest version of the position the blog
has been describing from the outside for six posts.

**And the objection survives it, because the objection is about who runs the check.** The instrument in Ernesto's
Law is the practitioner's own practising; the instrument in "validate your experiences" is the practitioner's own
framework. [The differential](/the-differential/)'s problem was never that a practitioner could not test anything;
it was that the faculty doing the testing is the faculty the state has taken. [A container](/the-container/) is an
instrument auditing itself. So the two positions are not in contradiction, and the honest form of this reading is
to hold both:

> **The book supplies a check, and the check is the practice. The blog's finding is that the practice is the
> instrument under audit — so the check is internal, complete, and exactly the kind of check the blog has learned
> not to count.**

Which is not a refutation of White. It is why §9's correction is to the **blog**: the blog's table of frames
assumed a frame either checks or cannot check, and this frame does neither. It checks, from inside, thoroughly —
and it is right to, if the cosmos is what it says it is. Whether the cosmos is what it says it is, is not a
question this series answers, and §7 shows what the book does with it.

## 7. The frame question: an aim, and no seat

The frames series ended on a three-part test — does the frame name the endpoint, is it checkable by someone who
does not benefit, does it leave a seat — and its tables record the living cosmos's endpoint as **none** and its seat
as **none**. Read against the book whole, that is half right, and the half it gets wrong is the interesting half.

**The book names no endpoint**, and it is explicit that the question is open. It poses it as a question: "At the
heart of the challenge is whether you believe a living universe has a **telos, a purpose.** You can nod along and
say the whole thing is alive, but do you think the earth is an open or closed system? Where does relationality
end?"[^telos] Where the book reports a tradition that carries teleological language, it marks the term as an
approximation: of the Ngarinyin and Woddordda creation complex Yorro Yorro, "'Something like' history, science and
telos, Yorro Yorro is both the deeds of original creation and their ceremonial marking and perpetuation."[^telos] No
named destination, then, and the question held open. That much the tables have right.

**But the book does name an aim** — which is not the same thing as an endpoint, and not nothing. The author's
definition of animism ends "toward the **cosmic aim of mutual flourishing**"; the working definition ends "the
business — busy-ness — of the animist to ensure and promote **right relations**"; the practice is summarised as
custodianship, and "Custodianship is not management because **we don't do it alone**"; and the instruction about
the world is directional: "We have to stop believing in a finished world and start thinking with an
**ever-finishing** one."[^telos][^anim] That is a telos-shaped claim with no terminus and no owner: not *the road
ends here*, but *the road is going somewhere, and everyone on it is walking*.

**And it leaves no seat, by construction** — the point the blog got right, made explicit here. If the aim is
mutual flourishing, the obligation custodial, and "we don't do it alone", there is no office at the end of the road
and no single voice that is the whole; the failure mode is **management**, "the position in which we perform the
worst", and the alternative is not a better manager.[^telos]

So the corrected entry reads: **no endpoint, no seat, and an aim held by everyone.** [A cosmos
of persons](/a-cosmos-of-persons/) as that post described it is plural and unowned with no named aim; [the cosmos
that refuses](/the-cosmos-that-refuses/) declines the highest term as a technique. Ani.Mystic does what neither
does — it makes a claim about the **direction** of the whole and then refuses to put anyone in charge of it. Which
is why the frame is not seat-shaped, and also why it cannot be checked by asking who holds the road, because the
answer is *everybody, and that is not a check either*.

## 8. The negative finding, and the counterweights, in the body

**The negative finding first, stated as a search result and scoped to the text of this edition.** The digital text of *Ani.Mystic*
contains **no clinical referral**. Case-insensitive searches return **0** hits for "safeguard", "safety",
"breakdown", "sanity", "psychosis", "seek help", "get help", "emergency", "suicide", "medication", "clinician" and
"counsel". Everything else clinical in the text is incidental, itemised at [^gap]: the "founding physicians" of
forest medicine; "the clinical impact of spending time in forests"; two proper nouns naming a cited researcher's
institutions; "botanists, doctors and anthropologists" as historical actors; and, among five hits for "therap", a
therapist in a passage on dream interpretation, a patient's therapist in an account of ancestral healing, a
"psychotherapist" in a citation, and "chemotherapy" twice. **No hit is a clinical referral**, and there is no
instruction anywhere in this book to take a symptom, a state or a crisis to anyone outside the practice. A search
shows only what it indexes, so the claim is scoped: within this text, the referral is absent.

**And there is no metered pace for the reader either** — no readiness test, no prerequisite training, no caution
about who should do this. This is the same gap [the holy daimon](/the-holy-daimon/) found in a practitioner's
manual, arriving from the cosmology's side; and as that reading said, **what is missing is not a pace. It is a
check.** What the *book* hands the reader is the book.

**Now the counterweights, and they are real.** The first is that the book reports its own practice candidly,
including the parts a less careful author would leave out — and one of those parts is a self-imposed limit that
functions as a safeguard even though the book does not call it one. Describing a growth-and-renewal ceremony for
newly planted cider apples, White writes that he had no ayahuasca to hand and, "more importantly, **not having any
training or authority in serving the medicine**", took "instead … a very high dose of LSD in the spirit room of my
farmhouse".[^gap] That is a distinction between *practising* and *serving medicine to others*, drawn by the author
about himself; it belongs in the body because the negative finding above could otherwise read as an author with no
such limit at all, and because the dose is reported with the same candour as everything else — a very high dose, in a farmhouse, in a book with no referral.

The second is the frame's humility, which is genuine and sits oddly with the diagnosis in §3:

> "My animism — whose borders are wholly contained by the four walls of the haunted library — makes no claims of
> universality, nor does it really offer explanations — belonging as they do to an earlier age."[^humble]

And, of what animism is "ultimately": "**Animist thinking is not the adoption of Indigenous theories of mind.** It
is the potential emergence of a theory of mind in relation to Indigenous thinkings when one becomes aware that there
might be ways to learn from Indigenous cosmovisions **without harvesting them.**"[^humble] The Introduction sets the
same boundary as method — "The days of externally describing the Other are mercifully over. **Full descriptive
autonomy belongs to everyone.**" — and confines the whole book to "animism as a 'western' framework".[^anim]

**Three things this post does not do.** It does not assess White or any practitioner; it does not validate or
disqualify anyone's experience, including the ones the book reports; and nothing here is grounds to restrict
anyone's practice. The levers are reported as **what the text contains and what it lacks**.

## 9. What the rest of the blog owes

Three corrections. The first is the one this post exists for.

**First: the frames' two-column table has no row for this.** The series sorted the cosmologies on two axes —
*checkable* and *plural and unowned* — and recorded the living cosmos as "not in the same way — a **real
weakness**".
As a summary of a *position*, that is accurate. As a description of *this book*, it is wrong in a way that matters:
**the book does not lack a check. It has one, and the check is participation** — and that is not the same as no
check, because of one property: **it is structurally identical to the practice it validates.** The doing is the
data channel, the loop that validates is the loop that practises. Which makes the correction narrow and sharp:

> **A frame can be checked from inside, thoroughly, by the practitioner and by nobody else — and that is not the
> same as being unchecked. It is worse in one specific way: it presents as a check.**

The guardrail is right, and this book demonstrates rather than refutes it — a frame "that cannot be checked from
outside" is exactly what the guardrail names. What the guardrail did not say is that such a frame will usually not
*feel* uncheckable, because it will have supplied a check. **The tell the blog should add is not the absence of a
method. It is the presence of a method whose instrument is the practitioner.** Every frames post can now run it, and
it is the same question the seat in [the owned cosmos](/the-owned-cosmos/) raised: who holds the thing that decides
whether this is working?

**Second: the frames' tables record the living cosmos's endpoint as "none", and it should read "an aim, no seat".**
§7 is the argument: the book names "the cosmic aim of mutual flourishing", "right relations", custodianship, an
ever-finishing world — and stations no one. Collapsing that into "none" loses what distinguishes this frame from
[the cosmos that refuses](/the-cosmos-that-refuses/), which declines the highest term as a technique. A frame can
have a vector and no terminus, and the blog's vocabulary does not currently carry that.

**Third: [the holy daimon](/the-holy-daimon/)'s finding now has a second instance from the other side.** That
reading found a Western text that keeps the endpoint unnamed, keeps the route plural, leaves no seat — "and it has
no check at all", so that the requirements are **independent**. *Ani.Mystic* is the same result at cosmological
scale, plus what §5 adds: not no check, but a check that is the practice. So the blog's own line — "the ontology
does not deliver the safeguard. The practice does" — acquires a sting. **Here the practice is the safeguard *and*
the check**, which means the blog has been using one word for two jobs: the discipline that keeps a frame from
being owned, and the discipline that decides whether it is working. This book is the case where one act does both —
a correction to how the blog talks about practice, not to the book.

## 10. What a reading is for

A reading holds one source **whole** — its definitions, its diagnosis, its epistemology, its omissions, its own
boundary statements — and asks what a blog that has quoted sources all along now has to change.

What this one says is that the blog has been quoting a frame it had already correctly identified and had not
finished reading. [The opener](/the-frame-is-a-variable/) and [a cosmos of persons](/a-cosmos-of-persons/) read
their two passages accurately, and neither adopted the cosmology; but a source used for two of its sentences comes
back as two sentences, floating free of the machinery holding them up. Put them back in and the book is not a
description of an enchanted world. It is an **argument about knowledge**, made from inside one — with a named enemy
(the one-way method), a named replacement (the encounter), a named method (continual contact), a named law
(Ernesto's), and a named era (Intermediatism, whose epistemology is expression and acceptance).

And its cost is exactly where the blog predicted, one scale up. The book does not need a seat because it does not
need an authority: the validation is the participating, and the participant is the validator. That is the strongest
available statement of a living cosmos, and the cleanest case in the blog of a frame that is **complete from the
inside and unreachable from the outside** — with a check in it, which is the part the blog had not seen. The
blog's contribution is not a verdict on whether the cosmos is alive. It is the sentence the book's own material
makes possible, and it belongs beside the guardrail:

> **A frame that cannot be checked from outside will usually supply a check. Ask who runs it.**
